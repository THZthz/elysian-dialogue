## Concept Combination Methods

The sharpest What-If concepts are often not a single concept but two unrelated concepts colliding to produce a chemical reaction.

### Method 1: Concept Grafting
Transplant one concept's rules into a completely unrelated domain.
**Process**: Take Concept A's operating mechanism + Concept B's application scenario = New concept.
Examples: "Elimination threshold" (elimination mechanism) + AI anxiety → "A company uses AI to score every employee; those below the execution threshold find only a USB stick on their desk the next day." Game respawn mechanic + workplace layoffs → "Laid-off workers can insert coins to respawn, but each respawn halves their salary."

### Method 2: Temporal-Spatial Dislocation
Put a character / technology / institution into an era or place it doesn't belong.
**Process**: Take person/technology/institution A + place into era/location B = collision.
The power lies in: temporal-spatial dislocation lets the audience simultaneously see two eras — understanding contemporary absurdity needs an ancient mirror, and vice versa.

### Method 3: Metaphor Concretization
Turn an abstract social anxiety into a physical rule that can be captured by a camera.
**Process**: Take abstract anxiety A → find its "smallest action" at the behavioral level → turn this action into a mandatory physical rule.
Examples: "Involution" minimum action is "others work overtime, so I must too" → physical rule: "Everyone's light must be brighter than their neighbor's; insufficient brightness means power cut."

### Method 4: Reversal Juxtaposition
Find two things that are superficially identical but essentially contradictory, and place them in the same scene so they illuminate each other's absurdity.
**Process**: Find two superficially similar but essentially contradictory things → let them coexist in the same scene.

### Method 5: Rule Reversal
Take a real-world rule and flip it — "what if this rule were reversed?"
**Process**: Find a rule people take for granted → flip it → extrapolate consequences.
Examples: "The older, the more mature" → flip: "People are born old, live younger, and die as infants." "The harder you work, the more you succeed" → flip: "In this world, the harder you work, the less you possess."

### Concept Combination Validation
- Does the combination produce a "1+1>2" effect? (If combining doesn't make it more interesting than either alone, abandon the combination.)
- Can the audience understand the combination's rules within 5 seconds?
- Does the combination point to a real social emotion or human weakness?
- Does the combination have extrapolation space — what happens when the rule is pushed to the extreme limit?
- Does the combination have reversal potential — can it finally slap the audience back?
