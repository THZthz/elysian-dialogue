## How-to-Tell Creative Methods

How-to-Tell doesn't change "what is told" but completely changes "how it is told." Its premise: the audience already knows (or can quickly understand) the story's content, so content itself is not the selling point — **the narrative method itself** is.

### Method 1: Perspective Substitution
Tell the story from an impossible perspective. Examples: from a bullet's perspective tell a single war; from a takeout bag's perspective tell a solitary person's day; from the Earth's perspective tell human civilization history; from an emoji's perspective tell its life of being sent, received, screenshot, forwarded.

### Method 2: Form Simulation
Repackage the story using an existing form (file format, interface, game, manual, etc.). Examples: using "product manual" format to tell a marriage; using "game walkthrough" format to tell a worker's day; using "app interface interaction" to tell a person's gradual social isolation; using "surveillance camera footage" to tell a convenience store love story; using "search history" to tell a person's psychological journey from diagnosis to acceptance.

### Method 3: Time Surgery
Perform surgery on time — accelerate, decelerate, reverse, loop, jump, juxtapose different times. Examples: compress one person's entire life into 2 minutes; stretch a 1-second action to 3 minutes; the same street corner over 100 years; simultaneously play forward one person's morning AND reverse the same person's night — the two timelines meet at noon.

### Method 4: Scale Jumping
Jump between the extremely large and extremely small; use scale contrast to create impact. Examples: start from a cell dividing → gradually pull out to a person → a city → a planet → a galaxy — then cut back to the cell. A family argument → cut to outer space looking at Earth → cut back to the argument → the argument suddenly becomes ridiculous.

### Method 5: Rule Constraint
Impose a rule constraint on the narration itself — it is precisely this constraint that generates creativity. Examples: the entire film uses only one fixed camera position, no editing; the entire film has not one person appearing — only spaces and objects; the entire film is only sound, the image is black (or only image, no sound); the entire film is one uninterrupted dolly shot; the entire film is composed of phone screenshots / chat records / notification bar.

### How-to-Tell Structure
- **Form Establishment (first 20-30s)**: Let the audience understand "what method is this work using to tell the story." The form itself should have freshness and appeal.
- **Form Deepening (middle 60-120s)**: Unfold content within the form's framework. As content progresses, the form itself also progresses — not repeating the same technique but the form escalating, mutating, or revealing new meanings.
- **Form-Content Convergence (final 15-30s)**: At some moment, the audience realizes form and content are one — "telling it this way" is not a flashy wrapping; it IS part of the story. The best How-to-Tell endings are where the form itself completes the reversal.
