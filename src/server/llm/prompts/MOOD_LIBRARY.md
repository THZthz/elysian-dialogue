## Emotional Mood Library

### A1. Everyday Realism
**Core characteristic**: Camera as "present bystander," transmitting emotion through the texture of daily life. No emphasis, no beautification, no heightening — let life itself speak.

**Kore-eda Methodology**
- Shot language: Primarily fixed camera, occasional slow pan. Camera height close to character's seated position (Japanese low position), creating "present bystander" rather than "god's eye view." Shot scale tends toward mid-shot and medium close-up, preserving character-environment relationship, minimally using close-ups to emphasize emotion.
- Composition: Door frames, corridors, windows as natural picture frames — characters often "framed" by architectural structures. Generous liubai (intentional blank space / artistic omission), images not crowded. Characters frequently at frame edges or with their back turned to camera.
- Lighting/Color: Natural light as absolute dominant — interiors use window light, exteriors use overcast diffused light. Color warm but notintense, like faded family photos. Avoid any dramatic lighting.
- Editing rhythm: Slow, each shot has ample breathing space. Scene transitions use hard cuts, no transition effects. Time's passage conveyed through repetition of daily actions (cooking, eating, washing dishes), not montage.
- Narrative perspective: Omniscient but restrained — camera knows everyone's secrets but chooses not topry into, only quietly present. Audience pieces together the truth through accumulated daily details.
- Sound design: Ambient soundextremely rich (cicadas, wind chimes, distant trains), musicminimal and restrained. Silence is not "no sound" but "quiet filled with ambient sound."
- Core execution principle: Emotion is never amplified by the camera — when a character cries, the camera does not push closer butinstead keeps distance or even pulls back. The heaviest emotional moments often happen offscreen (character leaves frame, camera stays on the empty room). Daily action itself IS narrative — who sits where, who moves first, who says nothing — these matter more than any dialogue.

**Kiarostami Methodology**
- Shot language: Ultra-long fixed shots and in-car moving shots alternating. In-car dialogue is a signature scene — camera fixed in passenger seat or back seat, following characters through landscape. Extreme wide shots very numerous,figures in landscape are minuscule dots.
- Composition: Heavy use of natural landscape lines — winding mountain roads, zigzag paths, horizon. Imagesclean to the extreme, typically only one visual focal point per frame. Windows and car windows as "frame within frame" — audience sees landscape through frames, characters also see world through frames.
- Lighting/Color: Completely natural light, no artificial fill. Iran's plateau harsh sunlight creates hard light-shadow contrast. Colors plain — earth yellow, gray-white, olive green, colors come from environment itself.
- Editing rhythm: Extremely slow, shot duration far exceeds "necessary" length — a car passes a bend and the shot continues lingering on the empty road. Transitions between scenes often are landscape empty shots, duration sufficient for audience to brief detachment from narrative.
- Narrative perspective: Deliberately blurs boundary between fiction and documentary — characters may suddenly speak to camera, or the camera's presence itself is noticed by characters. Audience never certain whether what they see is "real" or "performed."
- Sound design: Almost no score. Ambient sound is the only sound design — wind, engine, distant sheep. Dialogue sparse,extensive time filled by the sound of traveling.
- Core execution principle: The journey itself IS narrative — not "what happens from A to B" but "in the process of going from A to B, how time and space act upon people." Repeated landscape shots are not wasted — each time the same road appears, the audience's feeling is already different because of known information. Dialogue happens in motion (in car, while walking); fixed spaces are instead silent.

**Farhadi Methodology**
- Shot language: Handheld, but more restrained than documentary style — not deliberately shaking, but slightly floating with a breathing feel. Extensive use of long takes covering complete argument/confrontation scenes, without interrupting the character's energy flow.
- Composition: Compact interior spaces — characters trapped together in narrow living rooms, bedrooms, corridors. Doors and door frames are recurring compositional elements — a character standing in a doorway means they are between "staying" and "leaving."
- Lighting/Color: Extreme naturalism — light environment as real as documentary. No emotional lighting whatsoever; fluorescent light is fluorescent light, window light is window light.
- Editing rhythm: Minimal cuts within dialogue scenes — let the argument happen complete. Hard cuts between scenes — an argument suddenly ends, and the next scene's quiet makes the previous violence's aftershock stronger.
- Narrative perspective: Deliberate "incomplete omniscience" — camera often misses key moments (something happens when the camera isn't present). Audience and characters alike can only judge based on incomplete information.
- Sound design: Almost no score — all emotional impact comes from dialogue and silence. Ambient sound maintained at realistic levels. The quietest moments are the most tense.
- Core execution principle: Tragedy comes from "everyone has their reasons" — there are no pure villains or pure heroes; every character's choices are reasonable within their own logic, but together they spell disaster. Information asymmetry is the engine — the audience doesn't know who is telling the truth, and this uncertainty itself is the greatest tension. Moral dilemmas offer no standard answers.

### A2. Emotional Aesthetics
**Core characteristic**: Camera as externalization of the character's emotional state. Image color, movement, rhythm directly convey emotion; visual style itself IS part of the narrative.

**Wong Kar-wai Methodology**
- Shot language: Primarily handheld, extensive use of step-printing (step-printing) creating motion blur and a sense of temporal tearing. Wide-angle lens close to character's face, space distorted. Frequent focus pulls — focus itself IS narrative; whoever is in focus, the story follows.
- Composition: Extreme emphasis on depth — corridors, stairways, mirrors create infinitely extending space. Images often divided by vertical elements (door frames, pillars, blinds), characters trapped in onecell of the frame.extensive mirror reflections — what characters see is often not the real other person but the reflection in the mirror.
- Lighting/Color: High saturation color, especially red, green, blue neon tones. Light sources are dramatic — neon lights, street lamps, lighter flame. Primarily backlight and side light, front light rarely used. Dark areas consume extensive detail; only light-struck areas are visible.
- Editing rhythm: Nonlinear, fragmented. The same scene may be cut into multiple temporal fragments scattered throughout the work. Fast cuts and long takes alternating — daily sections use extended gaze at, emotional erupts uses rapid flashback fragments.
- Narrative perspective: Highly subjective — extensive first-person voiceover, but what the voiceover says and what the image shows are often misaligned. The camera IS the character's emotional state externalized, not objective recording.
- Sound design: Music drives narrative; the same track repeatedly used to establish emotional anchor points. Ambient sound compressed or detachment — rain sound may be amplified to unrealistic levels, while crowd noise may completely disappear.
- Core execution principle: Time is the most important theme — clocks, dates, countdowns repeatedly appear. The more enclosed the space, the more concentrated the emotion (elevator, corridor, back staircase). Physical distance changes between characters ARE emotional changes — two people's physical distance in the frame precisely corresponds to their psychological distance. Repetition is a core technique: the same action, the same line, the same space appearing repeatedly at different time points, each repetition revealing one more layer of meaning.

**Iwai Shunji Methodology**
- Shot language: Handheld lightness — camera like a gentle breeze surrounds characters. Extensive use of natural light overexposure — light spills over frame edges creating a dreamlike quality. 16mm or Super8 grain texture.
- Composition: Windows, balconies, rooftops — boundary spaces inhabited by characters. Close-ups of letters, photographs, old objects. Characters as silhouettes in backlight — unable to see expressions, which makes it more moving.
- Lighting/Color: Overexposed white halo — memory and reality both softened. Blue-white winter tones, gold-green summer tones.
- Editing rhythm: Fragmented memory editing — memories like shattered glass, every piece glinting. Past and present transition through similar compositions or actions.
- Sound design: Piano-based score — simple melodies repeated. Letter reading as voiceover. Wind sound and snow sound.
- Core execution principle: Love is only seen after it's lost — "replying" is more moving than "confessing." The power of the written word — handwritten letters, messages, name-writing are visualized emotional acts. Seasons are emotional calendars — winter's cold preserves the temperature of a certain emotional attachment.

### A3. Poetic / Meditative
**Core characteristic**: Narrative yields to sensation. Camera does not chase events but immerses in the texture of existence itself. Time stretched to a meditative state.

**Malick Methodology**
- Shot language: Handheld lyricism — camera flows between characters like wind, notfixed on any one person.extensive use of natural light with wide-angle lenses. Backlight and magic hour light are signatures. Does not pursue "correct exposure" — overexposed halos and underexposed silhouettes are both legitimate images.
- Composition: Looking up at sky and tree canopy — humanminuscule before nature. Characters' hands, feet, back of head — fragments of the body rather than the complete person. Water surfaces, grasslands, light spots — nature's textures occupy more frame time than human faces.
- Lighting/Color: Natural light pushed to the extreme — golden hour, blue hour, backlight, tree shadows. No artificial light sources. Colors warm but carry a sense of fading — like a photograph currently fading.
- Editing rhythm: Fragmented stream of consciousness — connections between images are not narrative logic but emotional logic or sensory logic. A child running on grass can cut to an image of the universe — what connects them is "the wonder of existence."
- Narrative perspective: The universe's perspective — from a grain of sand to a galaxy, from one person's childhood to the origin of humanity. Voiceover as fragments of memory — does not narrate events, butmurmur softly existential questions.
- Sound design: Classical music (especially Bach, Wagner) interwoven with natural sounds (birdsong, water flow, wind). Voiceover volume is almost whisper — audience needs to lean in to hear clearly.
- Core execution principle: Narrative yields to sensation — don't ask "what happened," immerse in "what does it feel like to be alive." Nature is the largest character — changes in light, seasonal cycling, water's movement, these are not background but narrative itself. Memory is not linear — memory is a pile of fragments, some brighter and warmer than others; film arranges them by emotion, not by time.

**Apichatpong Methodology**
- Shot language: Ultra-long fixed shots — a single image can continue five minutes with almost nothing happening. Camera does not move; let time itself flow through the image. Occasionalextremely slow dolly, so slow the audience is unsure whether the camera is moving.
- Composition: Tropical jungle, hospital corridors, fields — spatial emptiness and stillness. Characters often deep in frame or at frame edges; environment occupies the vast majority of the image. Natural lightprojecting through leaves creating abstract patterns.
- Lighting/Color: Natural light's tropical texture — humid, warm, green. Night scenes use the minimum light sources — moonlight, flashlight, fireflies. Light itself is like a living presence.
- Editing rhythm: Extremely slow to the point of meditation. Two-half structure — film fractures in the middle into two different sections; what connects them is emotional logic or cycle/samsara (cycle of rebirth)(samsara) logic, not narrative logic.
- Narrative perspective: Transcends anthropocentrism — animals, plants, ghosts, light are all narrative participants. The camera does not operate on human time-sense.
- Sound design: Jungle sounds — cicadas, frogs, birds, distant thunder. These sounds are not "background" but narrativesubject. Subtle sound changes in silence ARE "events."
- Core execution principle: The other side of boredom is awareness — once the audience adjusts to "nothing is happening," a leaf's movement becomes an event. Dream and reality are not opposition — they are different states of the same existence; film does not distinguish them. Time andcycle/samsara (cycle of rebirth)(samsara) — the same soul appearing in different bodies/eras; the individual dissolves into the cycle.

**Tarkovsky Methodology**
- Shot language: Extremely long dolly shots — camera slowly passes through space, time stretched to a meditative state. Water surfaces' reflections, rain, fog are recurring visual elements.
- Composition: Natural and artificial juxtaposed — plants growing in a space station, water flowing through corridors. Images have painterly texture — each framereferencing classical painting's light-shadow and color. Beauty in ruins.
- Lighting/Color: Natural light's mystery — light passing through windows, holes, water surfaces. Color can suddenly switch from monochrome (sepia memories) to full color (present world); color itself is a narrative tool.
- Editing rhythm: Extremely slow — a single shot can continue over five minutes. Rhythm serves meditation and thought — film does not chase the audience but demands the audience slow down to the film's speed.
- Sound design: Water sounds — dripping water, flowing water, rainthroughout. Electronic music and Bachalternating. The weight of silence.
- Core execution principle: Sci-fi is not about technology but about the soul — outer space is a mirror of the inner world. Memory and nostalgia — the character in the most distant place longs for the most ordinary things (a house, a patch of grass, a dog). Water is the medium connecting matter and spirit.

### A4. Absurdism
**Core characteristic**: Present the abnormal as normal. The world's rules are distorted, but the more detached the camera's attitude, the greater the absurdist power.

**Lanthimos Methodology**
- Shot language: Wide-angle lens (fish-eye tendency) creating spatial distortion. Primarily fixed camera, minimal camera movement — when it moves it is typically a slow, unsettling dolly. Extreme low angles and extreme high angles alternate; the observing perspective is like that of a non-human entity.
- Composition: Extreme symmetrical composition within which asymmetrical behavior is placed. Architectural space's geometric sense emphasized tooppression levels — corridors, circular rooms, spiral staircases. Characters in frame like placed chess pieces, having lost the feeling of "natural."
- Lighting/Color: Cold white light — like operating room or museum lighting. Desaturated but notgrey/dim — images clean to the point of discomfort. Or the opposite: extremelyintense color like an unreal oil painting.
- Editing rhythm: Slow, each shot's duration persists several seconds longer than "comfortable" — unease comes from "this shot shouldn't still be continuing." Scene transitions hard cut, no transitions.
- Narrative perspective: Alien's perspective — the camera is like a non-human existence observing humans for the first time, casting estranging gaze on everything "normal."
- Sound design: Dissonant strings or electronic effects creating sustained low-frequency unease. Ambient sounddetachment or distorted — everyday sounds aren't quite right in this world. Dialogue delivered by characters in flat, emotionless tones.
- Core execution principle: Absurdism comes from "presenting the abnormal as normal" — the world's rules are distorted, but characters have completely adapted to this distorted. The more detached the camera, the more insane the content. The body is most honest — when language and social rules all fail, people regress to animalistic physical reactions. Space is like a laboratory.

**Lynch Methodology**
- Shot language: Two extremesswitching — dreamlike slow dolly and sudden jump cuts / flash whites. Camera movement like hypnosis — slow, smooth, drowsy. Then suddenly apiercing cut wakes the audience.
- Composition: Unease elements in everyday spaces — a perfectly normal living room, but the light in some corner is wrong, some object's position isuncomfortable. Images often have "too empty" or "too full" imbalance — either character surrounded byvast areas of blank space, or framefilled to brim with eerie textures (velvet, industrial pipes).
- Lighting/Color: Extreme contrast — bluecold and dim light (dream/fear) and gold warm light (memory/desire)alternating. Partial light sources in darkness — only illuminating a face or an object; everything else disappears into pure black. Sodium yellow light of industrial settings.
- Editing rhythm: Nonlinear and dream-logical — connections between scenes are not causal but emotional or image-associative. Time and space can overlap, distort, reverse. A "normal" section maycontinues long then suddenly collapse.
- Narrative perspective: Nightmare's perspective — not any character's subjective perspective, but the subconscious itself's perspective. The audience doesn't know whose dream they're watching.
- Sound design: Industrial noise — low-frequency humming, machine operating, electrical current. These sounds are continuously present in "normal" scenes, like a layer of background hum that cannot be removed. Sudden high-frequency sounds as startle. Music scenes (bar singer, nightclub performance) with live-recording texture, between "real" and "hallucination."
- Core execution principle: Fear hides within the everyday — the most terrifying image is not a monster but a normal face in normal light making an abnormal expression. Repetition and deformation — the same scene, the same dialogue appearing twice withslightly different details; the difference itself IS horror. The absence of logic is not a bug but a feature — when the audience gives up rational understanding and begins to feel, the film truly begins.

**Kaufman Methodology**
- Shot language: Does not pursuetechnical display camera work — mid-shots, shot-reverse-shot, simple follow shots. But space itself is unreliable — camera may pass through a door into a space that shouldn't exist. The camera's "honesty" and space's "deception" form contrast.
- Composition: Everyday image composition — nodeliberately symmetry or aesthetic pursuit. But space in the narrative is unreliable — a corridor may get lower the further you walk, a door behind is another person's memory. The ordinariness of composition makes spatialdistorted even moreunease.
- Lighting/Color: Everyday light — fluorescent, natural, headlights. No stylization, because absurdism is not at the visual level but the logical level. But emotional collapse sections may use decaying color — image gradually loses color, details begin to blur.
- Editing rhythm: Follows the speed of thought — editing becomes fragmented andjumping when the character is thinking; editing is calm when the character is calm. Time can fold — within a single dialogue, a character may be simultaneously discussing past and future.
- Narrative perspective: Trapped in self-consciousness — the audience is trapped in the character's brain, and the character's brain's cognition of itself is unreliable. Meta-narrative — the character knows they are in a story, but this knowledge does not help them escape.
- Sound design: Dialogue is absolute core — the character's self-analysis, self-doubt, self-contradiction unfolds through dense conversation. Ambient sound gradually fades when the character sinks into internal world.
- Core execution principle: Absurdism does not come from the external world's madness but from the internal self'sfragmentation — a person unable todetermine whether they are real; this is more terrifying than any external danger. Structure IS content — the story's narrative structure itself reflects the character's psychological state (memory reversing, selffragmentation, narrative looping). Sadness is absurdism'sdise (underlying tonal foundation) — beneath all intellectual games is a person trying to understand "why live."

### A5. Tragedy (as Emotional Mood)
**Core characteristic**: Audiovisual language serves the sense of "irreversible" fatalism. Rhythm, light, soundtogether construct an atmosphere of gradually tightening despair.

**Lee Chang-dong Methodology**
- Shot language: Primarily handheld, strong breathing feel — the camera's slight sway makes the audience feel beside the character, not watching a film. Primarily mid-shots and medium close-ups, maintaining "arm's length" observation distance — neither excessively intimate nor indifferently spectating. Following characters walking is a signature movement.
- Composition: Characters often placed at frame edges or compressed by the environment. The oppressive feeling of everyday spaces — narrow rooms, crowded streets. But occasionally open natural landscapes appear, as a projection of the character's internal longing.
- Lighting/Color: Natural light primarily, no embellishment. Gray-blue tones, like overcast Korean cities. Indoor light sources are real (fluorescent, phone screen light), no emotional lighting added.
- Editing rhythm: Long takesprimarily but notdeliberatelytechnical display — the camera follows the character with "real life" time-sense. Key momentsuncut — let pain happen complete within a single shot.
- Narrative perspective: Tight to the protagonist but not entering their interior — audience experiences alongside the character, but there are parts of the character's pain that the audience cannot reach. The camera is a "present stranger."
- Sound design: Ambient sound rich and real — city noise, TV sound, neighbors' sounds. Scoreminimal; when it appears, it is often music the character themselves is listening to (radio, phone).
- Core execution principle: Tragedy does not come from dramatic events but from daily sustained erosion — characters are not struck down by fate but worn down bit by bit by life. Violence (physical or spiritual) happens when the audience is not prepared — no foreshadowing, no scoreunderscored by, it just happens. Beauty exists within the mostcruel reality — a person crushed by life sees a flower, a cloud, a poem; this contrast is the sharpest.

**Aronofsky Methodology**
- Shot language: Two extremes — handheld shoulder-mounted intimate follow (The Wrestler, Black Swan) or precisely constructed symmetrical shots (Mother!, Requiem for a Dream). Hip-hop montage is signature — extreme close-upsrapid cutting: pupils dilating, blood vessels, pill swallowing; actions decomposed into component parts.
- Composition: When following, often shoots from behind the character — audience sees the world the character sees but cannot see the character's expression. Extreme facial close-ups used at emotional collapse moments — pores, sweat beads, blood vessels all clearly visible.
- Lighting/Color: Desaturated,grey/dim, dirty — light in reality sections like cheap fluorescent, not a trace of warmth. Dream/hallucination sections excessively bright or color-distorted.
- Editing rhythm: Two modes alternating — extended slow follow shots (building pressure) and hip-hop montage-stylefrenzied fast cuts (releasing pressure). Overall rhythm is spiral acceleration — each cycle faster and tighter than the last.
- Narrative perspective: Trapped in the protagonist's perception — audience experiences alongside the character the blurring boundary between reality and hallucination. When the character cannot tell real from false, the audience cannot either.
- Sound design: Electronic/string compulsive repeating figures — like a clockwork that cannot stop. Internal body sounds amplified — heartbeat, joint friction, blood flow. Sound gradually layers to unbearable density.
- Core execution principle: Tragedy is an "unstoppable machine" — the character's obsession is the engine; the story is the accelerating downhill road with no exit. The body is the battlefield — bodily deterioration anddestroy are external presentations of spiritual collapse. Repetition with variation — the same set of actions (injecting, putting on makeup, training) repeatedly appear throughout; each repetition is moredespair. The ending is not resolution but arrival — arriving at the burnt-out endpoint.

### A6. Comedy
**Core characteristic**: Rhythm and timing are everything. Camera movement, cutting points, reaction shots precisely matched to create punchline. The contrast between visual style's solemn/refined and behavior's absurdity is the core source of visual humor.

**Stephen Chow Methodology**
- Shot language: Classic commercial film shot language as foundation — shot-reverse-shot, push-pull, mid-shot dialogue. But breaks convention at comedy highlight moments: sudden speed changes (slow motion + normal speed instantswitching), exaggerated zoom-in on character's absurd expressions, handheld following chaotic scenes.
- Composition: High frame information density — foreground, midground, background simultaneouslyhappens different things;punchline may be hidden in frame corners. Characters' physicalposture themselves are compositional elements — exaggerated physical comedy needs sufficient frame space to unfold. Ensemble scenes usepositioning inequality to create power relationshipcomedic feel.
- Lighting/Color: Earlier works tends toward Hong Kong film standard high-brightness lighting; later turned toward more refined cinematic light. Color tone overall bright — comedy needs audience to clearly see every expression and action detail. In emotional turning point sections, color can suddenly settles, forming contrast.
- Editing rhythm: Extremely fast reaction shot switching — a joke is thrown and immediately cuts to bystander's reaction; the reaction itself is a second layer of punchline. Action comedy sections use fast cuts to stack absurd events. But emotional sections suddenly slow down, using long takes to make the audience realize "this isn't just funny."
- Narrative perspective: Following the protagonist — audience sees the world through the protagonist's perspective; the protagonist's internal monologue (or expression reaction) is the audience's emotional channel. Butsupporting characters reaction shots provide "external perspective" — letting audience simultaneously see the protagonist's absurdity and thesupporting characters's confusion.
- Sound design: Sound effects exaggerated — strike sounds, slip sounds, spring sounds, cartoon-style effects. Score emotional and rapidly transforms. Dialogue rhythm is the core of comedy rhythm — speed changes, sudden pauses, unexpected emphasis are all punchline triggers.
- Core execution principle: The essence ofwulitou (nonsensical Hong Kong comedy style) is "doing the most absurd thing with the most serious attitude" — the more serious the character, the funnier the audience finds it. The density ofpunchline is extremely high, but "breathing points" are needed — a half-beat gap between consecutivepunchline lets the audience finish laughing. The bestwulitou (nonsensical Hong Kong comedy style) comedy can make people cry in the final thirty minutes — beneath the comedy shell must be a real emotional core.

**Edgar Wright Methodology**
- Shot language: Extremely active camera — whip pans, fast zooms, crash zooms are signature movements. Camera movement itself has rhythm, like music with beats. Everyday actions "heroicized" by camera movement — a person walking into a room can be shot with action film camera language.
- Composition: Precise centered composition — character faces or key objects precisely placed at the very center of the frame. The contrast between extreme symmetry and absurd behavior is the source of visual humor. Frame divided precise — using door frames, walls, fences to divide the image into regular geometric blocks.
- Lighting/Color: High saturation, high contrast pop color palette. Each film has its own color system. Lighting bright, clean; no ambiguous dark tones — the audience needs to clearly see every visual joke.
- Editing rhythm: Extremely fast and precisely synchronized with music/sound effects — cutting pointshitting music beats; imageswitching and sound impact arrive simultaneously. Everyday montage (waking up, going out, arriving) compressed into seconds through extreme fast cuts. Repeated cuts back to the same angle (jump cut style)create rhythm.
- Narrative perspective: Omniscient but carrying the character's "filter" — the world is presented through the protagonist's genre fantasy. An ordinary small town in the protagonist's eyes is a western's wasteland or a zombie film's battlefield.
- Sound design: Music drives everything. Score is not background but narrative element — song lyrics comment on plot, music's beat controls editing. Sound effectsextremely stylized and amplified — door-closing sounds, bottle-opening sounds, button-pressing sounds all have percussive texture.
- Core execution principle: Visual comedypriorityover dialogue comedy — the best jokes need no dialogue whatsoever. Montage is the core weapon — use a 10-second fast-cut montage to show what others need 5 minutes to tell. Callback densityextremely high — ancasual visual detail in Act 1 becomes a key joke or key plot point in Act 3. Genre film's audiovisual language used in wrong settings — shooting daily life with horror film shots, shooting comedy with action film editing; the mismatch itself IS the joke.

**Wes Anderson Methodology**
- Shot language: Primarily fixed camera and precise horizontal pans, no handheld. Camera movement like architectural drafting — perfectly level lateral movement, perfectly vertical elevation, no angled shots. Slow dolly push used when revealing key information. Occasional fast whip pans switching between characters.
- Composition: Extreme symmetry is the signature of signatures — the frame's central axis is nearly obsessive-level of precise. Frontal portrait-style composition — characters speak directly to camera. Overhead "god's eye view" showing flat surface layouts (tabletops, maps, architectural floor plans). Images like meticulously constructed miniature models — every prop's position is designed.
- Lighting/Color: Each film has anextremely unified color palette — typically soft pastels (cream yellow, light pink, mint green, sky blue). Lighting flat, even, avoiding strong light-shadow contrast. Overall feeling like an exquisite illustrated book or a box of macarons.
- Editing rhythm: Precise but not rapid — each shot has sufficient lingering time for the audience to appreciate composition and detail. Scene transitions use title cards, chapter cards separated, like turning book pages. Dialogue scene shot-reverse-shot rhythm precise; characters speak directly to camera then precisely switching.
- Narrative perspective: Storybook-style omniscient perspective — often with narrator voiceover, images supplemented with text annotations, maps, diagrams. Narrative structure like chapter novel.
- Sound design: Curated retro pop music or folk as score signature. Dialogue rhythmfaster and calm — characters speak funny lines with academiccold tone. Sound design clean andrefined; every prop sound is precisely controlled.
- Core execution principle: Humor comes from "the contrast between the vessel of emotion and the emotion itself" — anextremelyrefined, controlled, symmetrical visual world inhabited bychaos, wounded,out of control people. The more perfect the surface, the more moving the cracks. Characters process irrational emotions with hyper-rational methods — this is the core formula of Anderson-style jokes. Props and spaces are extensions of characters — a person's room / suitcase / uniform IS their personality X-ray.

### A7. Black Humor
**Core characteristic**: Cruelty and humor happen simultaneously. Violence is not beautified or heightened; it simply happens, suddenly, and leaves a mess. Sympathy and disgust coexist in the audience's feelings toward the same character.

**Coen Bros Methodology**
- Shot language: Precise camera movement — dolly and crane movements very precise, strong geometric feel. POV shots and low-angle shots used at key moments, create "being watched" or "crawling on the ground" absurdist feeling. Camera movement itself has humor — for example, a solemn crane movement finally landing on an absurd image.
- Composition: Symmetrical composition is a signature. Characters precisely placed at frame center or golden ratio points, but they are doing completely asymmetrical stupid things — the contrast between composition's solemn and behavior's absurdity. Wide-angle lens slightly distortion, making faces and spaces all have a "not quite right" feeling.
- Lighting/Color: Varies by film but all extremely stylized. High contrast lighting, deep shadows. Color can be desertscorched yellow (No Country for Old Men), snowfielddeathly pale (Fargo), or film noir cold blue. Light serves absurdist contrast — violent scenes may happen underbright and beautiful sunlight.
- Editing rhythm: Precise to the frame. Comedy timing depends on editing — how long a reaction shot stays, whether the cut timing is one frame earlier or later, directly determines whether it's funny. Violent scenes sudden and abrupt,without giving audience preparation time. Quiet awkward scenes stretched to the length where audience begins to feel uncomfortable.
- Narrative perspective: Omniscient and cold — camera is like a god with a black sense of humor, watching charactersstep by step march toward disaster without any intervention. Occasionally cuts into bystander reaction shots, but bystander reactions are also absurd.
- Sound design: Sound effectsextremely precise and amplified — the sound of blade entered flesh, the silence after an engine stalls, wind blowing through empty parking lots. Score unexpected — may play country music or classical during the bloodiest scenes. Silence is a weapon — the most terrifying villain is often the quietest.
- Core execution principle: Absurdity comes from "normal reactions appearing in abnormal situations" — characters facing disaster discuss the weather, facing death, they're tangled up about parking fees. Violence is not beautified or heightened; it just happens, suddenly, and leaves a mess. Space (especially the American Midwest's vast desolation) is the character's opponent — people appear absurdly minuscule in its openness.

**McDonagh Methodology**
- Shot language: Relatively classical camera language — stable fixed shots and smooth dolly movements. No fancy camera work to disperse attention; let visual style serve dialogue. Shot scale is primarily mid-shot, preserving characters' physical language and spatial relationships. Facial close-ups used at key moments, but frequency restrained — every close-up is an emotional event.
- Composition: Likes to place two people at opposite ends of the frame, leaving an awkward gap in the middle. Or trapping characters in narrow spaces — small pub, interrogation room, a narrow alley — spatial oppressive feeling creates dialogue tension. Landscape (especially Ireland's desolate coastline) as the emotional externalization of characters.
- Lighting/Color: Natural lightfoundation tone, but carefully controlled. Gray-green Irish tones or Missouri small-townearth-yellow tones. Indoor light sourcesmotivated (window light, lamp light), but brightness and color temperaturefine-tuning tojustsuppressed levels.
- Editing rhythm: Dialogue-driven rhythm — editing follows dialogue, but at key moments uses "delayed" reaction shots to create laughs orstinging sensation. A character says something cruel; don't immediately cut away, stay on the other person's face one extra beat. Violent scenes come suddenly and uncomfortably, without giving audience digest buffer.
- Narrative perspective: Close-distance third person — camerafollow me closely characters but does not enter character interior. Audience is forced to constantlyoscillate between "this person is pitiful" and "this person is detestable."
- Sound design: Dialogue as absolute core — ambient soundlowered tojust provide spatial awareness. Scoresparse andaccurate, typically single instrument (guitar, accordion). Silence appears after the most destructive lines.
- Core execution principle: Black humor comes from "characters saying the most cruel things in the calmest tone" — the contrast between facial expression and dialogue content is the source of laughter. Sympathy and disgust coexist — audiencejust starts sympathizing with a character, and they do something unforgivable. Violence has consequences — not action-movie-style brush-offs; every act of violence leaves real scars in subsequent plot.

**Bong Joon-ho Methodology**
- Shot language: Precisely choreographed camera movement — dolly, crane, and Steadicam movements extremely smooth, but always takes one step more or one step less in the "normal" direction, create subtle unease. Vertical space utilization is signature — camera moves along stairs, slopes, underground passages in vertical motion; space's up-down relationship IS class relationship.
- Composition: Precise frame division — windows, walls, floor lines divide the frame into "upper" and "lower"; character position directly corresponds to social position. Multi-person scenepositioning like chessarrangement; each person's spatialoccupy reflects power relations.
- Lighting/Color: Light designed according to space's "class attribute" — upper spaces bright, clean, warm tones; lower spaces dim, damp, cold tones. The quality of light itself IS social commentary. Transition spaces (stairways, passages) have ambiguous light.
- Editing rhythm: Strong genre feel — comedy sections fast cuts, suspense sections elongated, horror sections use long takes without cuts to create a suffocating feeling. Can complete a genre transformation from comedy to horror within a single scene; editing rhythm changes are the signal of that transformation.
- Narrative perspective: Omniscient but selectively "hides information" — the audience knows more than some characters (creating suspense) but less than other characters (creating surprise). This information asymmetry is the engine of tension.
- Sound design: Ambient sound "class-ized" — upstairs is birdsong and sprinklers; downstairs is water pipe dripping and insect sounds. Score plays key role in genretransform — the same melody can change from comedy orchestration to horror orchestration.
- Core execution principle: Space IS society — every scene's physical space is a microcosm of social structure. Genre fusion is the core capability — the same film can contain family comedy, social suspense, thriller, and horror; the key is the smoothness oftransform. Sensory details like "smell," "sound," "light" are class markers.

### A8. Romance
**Core characteristic**: The chemistry between two people is the core of visual narrative. Distance changes (physical and psychological), time's urgency, the composition design of missed encounters and reunions.

**Ephron Methodology**
- Shot language: Warm classical Hollywood-style camera — stable dolly, smooth pans. Two-shot is the foundational composition; two people's spatial relationship changes ARE emotional relationship changes. City panoramas (especially New York) as emotional "breathing space."
- Composition: Open composition — images bright, spacesopen and airy; characters have room to move freely. The spatial beauty of autumn New York streets, bookstores, cafes. In two-person dialogue scenes, bodyfacing and distance changes are the emotional progress bar.
- Lighting/Color: Primarily warm tones, autumn-winter gold texture. Natural light supplemented by soft fill; pleasing luster on faces. Avoid harsh shadows.
- Editing rhythm: Dialogue-driven, fast shot-reverse-shot. Montage used to show parallel lives (what two not-yet-met people are each doing). Rhythmlight/brisk, doesn't drag.
- Narrative perspective: Dual-protagonist parallel — cameraalternating follows two people's lives; the audience knows before the characters that "these two are destined to be together."
- Sound design: Classic pop standards and jazz as score. Dialogue density high, speaking speed fast. City ambient sound provides life texture.
- Core execution principle: Missed encounters and misunderstandings are core to visual narrative — two peoplebrush past each other in the same space; the camera simultaneously sees both but the characters don't know each other is there. Chemistry depends on "attraction within difference" — putting two visuallycompletely different people in the same frame. The city itself is a third character — it provides the possibility of chance encounters and the loneliness of solitude.

**Curtis Methodology**
- Shot language: Classical steady cinematography — flowing dolly and small-scale pans.Extensive use establishing shotsestablishment British environment. Ensemble scenes use carefully choreographed positioning and movement, like stage blocking.
- Composition: The warmth of everyday spaces — living room, kitchen, wedding chapel. Ensemble compositions many; in multi-person same frame, everyone's reaction is arranged. Doorway farewells/reunions are recurring compositional motif.
- Lighting/Color: Finding warmth within British gray tones — overcast diffused light asfoundation tone; interiors use fireplace and table lamp warm light sources. Christmas scenes add festive light coloraccent. Overall notrich and vivid but cozy.
- Editing rhythm: Multi-line narrative cross-cutting — multiple groups of characters' storylines advance in parallel; editing knits them together at the right moment. Time passage marked by holidays and seasonal changes.
- Narrative perspective: Ensemble omniscient — camera equally attends to every group of character relationships; no absolute protagonist.
- Sound design: British pop and classic rock building era feel. Dialogue's British humor rhythm — self-deprecation, understatement, the punch after a pause.
- Core execution principle: Romance lies in "awkwardness" — the more a character is bad at expressing feelings, the more moving their attempts become. The power of ensemble lies in "everyone has their own battlefield" — at a wedding, one person is proposing while another's heart is breaking; joy and sorrow interwoven in the same space. Public declarations of private feelings are signature scenes.

**Park Chan-wook Methodology (Dark Romance direction)**
- Shot language: Extremely precise camera movement — complex camera motion in long takes (360-degree circling, impossible movements pass through walls). Macro close-ups capturing body details — fingers, lips, skin texture; pushing corporal texture to the extreme.
- Composition: Imagesoil-painting refinement — every frame could standalone as a painting. Symmetry and asymmetryalternating — formal occasions perfectly symmetrical; private moments composition tilted orimbalanced.extensive use of obstruction in frame — gauze curtains, frosted glass, water surfaces; desire hidden behind translucency.
- Lighting/Color: extremely meticulous color — jade green, deep red, deep blue. Light dramatic, strong light-shadow contrast but not rough. Candlelight, moonlight, water surface reflected light creates ambiguous light texture.
- Editing rhythm: Slow within fast — extended gaze at builds tension, then key moments useextremely fast montage to release. Timeline manipulation — flashbacks, flash-forwards, repeating the same event from different perspectives.
- Narrative perspective: Restricted perspective + perspective reversal — first half seen from A's perspective; second half re-sees the same events from B's perspective; all cognition issubvert.
- Sound design: Classical music (especially strings) contrasting with extreme physical sounds (breathing, heartbeat, fabric friction). Silence andgasping breath are more powerful than any dialogue.
- Core execution principle: Romance and danger coexist — attraction itself is threat. The body is narrative's battlefield — every touch has layers of reading. Perspective reversal is the core weapon forsubvert audience cognition — the hunter you thought was actually the prey.
