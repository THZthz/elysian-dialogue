## Action & Conflict Library

### C1. Modern Action
**Core challenge**: Clarity of action, spatial choreography, physical realism of impact.

**Stahelski Methodology**
- Shot language: Wide shots showing full-body action — not relying on fast cuts toconceal; let audience see complete body movement. Multi-opponent combat in long takes — cameradarting through through combatbutuncut; action choreography and execution happen in continuous time and space.
- Composition: Space used as combat tool — stairs, corridors, library bookshelves allparticipates in combat. Every environment has unique spatial rules; combat style changes with space.
- Lighting/Color: Neon colors — blue, red, purple extreme colorsform visual identity. Night scenesprimarily; city lights are primary light source.
- Editing rhythm: Minimal cuts within action sections — one long take can cover multiple opponents' combat. Each strike's impact conveyed through real reaction time and sound, not fast cuts.
- Sound design: Strike sounds' bone feel — every punch, everythrow has weight. Gunshots at real volume levels. Electronic music score.
- Core execution principle: Action clarity — the audience must be able to understand what happened. Physical authenticity — characters get tired, injured, bleed. World-building conveyed through action — different factions have different combat styles.

**Woo Methodology**
- Shot language: Slow motion is core — hero's key actions magnified by slow motion into visual poetry. Multi-angle coverage — the same action shot from three or four angles then cut together. Camera does arcing movement around characters,weaving through flying doves and shell casings.
- Composition: Dual-gun symmetry — character's dual-wielding posture is signature composition. Dove, candle, crucifix as religious imagery. Confrontation composition — two people facing each other; space permeated smoke.
- Lighting/Color: Backlit hero silhouettes — characters standing before light sources outlined. Light beams in smoke. Warm tones for brotherhood sections; cool tones for confrontation sections.
- Editing rhythm: Slow motion and normal speedalternating — rhythm like music's strong and weak beats. Normal-speedchaotic combat suddenly cuts into slow-motion "hero moment."
- Sound design: Gunshots stylized — not pursuing realism but rhythm. The score is emotionally heightened. Bullets pass through the sound of space.
- Core execution principle: Violence IS aesthetics — action is not just narrative function but independent visual poetry. Brotherhood — respect between opponents matters more than victory or defeat. Ritual — the final duellike a religious ceremony.

**Miller Methodology**
- Shot language: Frame center rule — key visual informationalways at frame center, because at high speed the audience's eyes can onlystaring at center. This rule allows the audience to still comprehend at extreme cutting speeds.
- Composition: Motion vectors — movement direction within the frame precisely controlled; audienceattention follows movement direction. Foreground flying dust and debris increase speed sensation.
- Lighting/Color: Desert orange-yellow and sky blue — high-contrast dual-color system. Metal reflections and flame orange light.
- Editing rhythm: Extremely fast but readable — each shotonlyconveyed one unit of information; fast cutting only accelerates information transmission speed, notconfuse information. Rhythmlike percussion — has strong beats (explosions, collisions) and weak beats (character reactions).
- Sound design: Engine sounds are the main melody — different vehicles have different sound "personalities." Metalcollision, explosions, windform symphony.
- Core execution principle: Visual narrative — dialogue minimal; all information transmitted through image and action. Practical shooting priority — real vehicles, real stunts, real dust have more impact than CG. Chase IS narrative — not "telling a story during a chase" but "the chase itself IS the story."

### C2. Wuxia
**Core challenge**: Poeticization of action,yijing (artistic conception / poetic atmosphere) of space, correspondence between technique and character interior.

**Ang Lee Methodology (Wuxia dimension)**
- Shot language: Flying camera — in wire-work action, camera follows characters flying between rooftops and bamboo forests. Action scenes dance-ified — technique choreographylike ballet. Long takesshow continuous action sequences.
- Composition: Landscape painting-style scenery — characters battle in waterfalls, bamboo forests, deserts; nature is the largest set. Vertical space utilization — from ground to rooftop to sky.
- Lighting/Color: Natural light's Chinese landscape texture — morning mist, sunset, moonlight. Color tonessubdued/elegant — ink-wash style desaturation.
- Editing rhythm: Action sections alternating between flowing long takes and precise fast cuts — acoherent flight sequence followed by sudden precise close-ups.
- Sound design: Tan Dun-style East-West fusion score — cello and erhu. Weaponcollision metal sounds stylized. Wind and fabricbillowing sounds.
- Core execution principle: Wuxia's "xia" — combat is not for victory but for a certain conviction. Action IS interior — every technique choice reflects the character's current psychological state.suppressed emotion released through combat — two people fighting may be more intimate than two people embracing.

**Tsui Hark Methodology**
- Shot language: Creativity-priority camera movement — camera can be tied to a sword, placed under horse hooves,weaving through flames. Perspectiveunconstrained by physical limitation. Fast zooms and whip pans increase chaotic energy feel.
- Composition: Extreme spatial utilization — narrow alleys, inn staircases, tavern multi-level structures; combat uses every inch of space. High visual density — multiple things happening simultaneously in the frame.
- Lighting/Color:intense colors — red lanterns, gold Buddha statues, emerald bamboo forests. Extreme light-shadow contrast — sunlightweaving through architectural gaps in strong beams. Smoke and flying sandcreate volumetric light.
- Editing rhythm: Extremely fast — fragmented fast cutscreatedazzleddazzling speed inchaotic combat. But key "kill shot" can suddenly slow, letting one sword strike's weight land.
- Sound design: Weapon soundsextremely stylized — sword wind, hidden weaponpiercing the air, palm wind. Scoreintense. Battle cries.
- Core execution principle: Imagination is the first weapon — wuxia's "kung fu"unconstrained by physical law limitation. World-building richness — every detail (menu, street, shop sign) constructs a living jianghu. Speed and density — high information volume, fast rhythm, without giving breathing space.

**Hu Jinquan Methodology**
- Shot language: Fixed camera + precise character blocking — opera-style blocking; character entrances and exitslike stage entrances and exits.overhead shot andlow-angle shot ritualism. Long takes in bamboo forests, inns, mountain paths.
- Composition: Beijing opera stage spatial sense — minimalist space; narrative carried by character movement andpositioning. Natural landscape as "heaven and earth" presence — humanminuscule but dignified between heaven and earth.
- Lighting/Color: Natural light — sunlight through bamboo leaves, morning mist on mountain paths, sunset at ancient temple. Color subdued/elegant,approaching ink-wash.
- Editing rhythm: Beijing opera-style rhythm — still, still, still, explode. Extended confrontation and waiting followed by instantaneouserupt. The action itself is extremely short — one strike settlesvictory or defeat.
- Sound design: Gong and drum points — Beijing opera martial-scene percussion rhythm applied in editing. Ambient soundemptinesssense — wind, water, birdsong.
- Core execution principle: Wuxia's liubai (intentional blank space / artistic omission) — the highest-level combat lies not in quantity of moves but in one strike's weight. Space IS yijing (artistic conception / poetic atmosphere) — bamboo forest is not just a combat venue but the externalization of a certain spiritual state. The solitude of xia — the true xia always faces alone.

### C3. War
**Core challenge**: Visual contrast between chaos and order, individualminuscule within collective violence.

**Spielberg Methodology (War dimension)**
- Shot language: Handheld chaos — making the audience feel they are on the battlefield. Fast pans following soldiersweaving through bullet rain. Camerastained with mud, water drops, blood.
- Composition: Momentary order within chaos — in complete collapse, one soldier's facial close-up suddenly quiets everything.
- Lighting/Color: Desaturated film texture — approaching combat photography realism. Gray-green tones.
- Editing rhythm: Battle sections extremely fast and chaotic — the audience, like soldiers, cannot process all information. But between battles suddenly slows — silence is more terrifying than gunfire.
- Sound design: Bullet sound directionality — the audience can hear where bullets come from. Tinnitus after explosions. Soldiers'gasping breath and shouting.
- Core execution principle: War is not a heroism stage but a hell of fear. Personal perspective — seeing big history through one small person's eyes. Survival is not victory — the living are not better than the dead, only luckier.

**Nolan Methodology (War dimension)**
- Multi-timeline cross-cutting — land, sea, airthree lines lines advancing on different time scales (one week, one day, one hour) simultaneously edited. Time compressioncreate urgency. IMAXactually shot physical impact — plane engine sounds, waves, shells' somaticvibration.

**Mendes Methodology**
- Shot language: "Pseudo-oner" — entire filmlike one uninterrupted long take; camera follows soldierspass through battlefield. Time and space unfold in one continuous experience.
- Composition: Trench depth — ahead is unknown death; behind is the path taken. Characteralways advancing in frame. Beauty in ruins — bombed churches, rivers lit by flares at night.
- Lighting/Color: One day's light changes — fromearly morning to evening to dawn; natural light changes mark time and emotion. Night scenes only use in-scene firelight and flares.
- Editing rhythm: "No cutting" — pseudo-long take achieved through hiddenhidden transition; audience's time experience synchronizes with character's time experience.
- Sound design: 360-degree ambient sound surround — audience hears everything the character hears, no more, no less. Sudden gunfire startles audience and character simultaneously.
- Core execution principle: Real-time experience — without giving audience detachment opportunity. Task simplicity and execution difficulty — "deliver a letter," this simple task becomes an impossible journey on the battlefield. Time IS life — every second, someone is dying.

### C4. Disaster
**Core challenge**: Presentation of scale, individualminuscule before natural forces.

**Emmerich Methodology**
- Shot language:extensive VFX panoramasshow disaster scale — cities submerged, buildings collapse, groundsplit open. Low-anglelow-angle shot disasterapproaching — making audience feel "pressing down" fear.
- Composition: Human vs natural force contrast — humanextremely small while disasterenormous. Iconic landmark destruction — familiar landmarks destroyedcreate impact.
- Lighting/Color:grey/dim — dust, smoke, stormblocked sky. Occasional sunlightpenetrated — representing hope.
- Editing rhythm: Slow setup before disaster,extremely fast during disaster. Multi-line cross-cutting — people in different locations simultaneously facing disaster.
- Sound design: Low-frequencyroar — earthquake, tsunami, storm's somatic sound. Human screaming swallowed byimmense natural sounds.
- Core execution principle: Scale staircase — disasterlevel after level larger. Family is microcosm of human society — protecting family IS protecting humanity. Disaster's democracy — all classes are on the same boat.

**Cuarón Methodology (Disaster/Survival dimension)**
- Shot language: Ultra-long takes (over ten minutesuncut)create real-time immersion. Camera 360-degree rotation inweightless environment.
- Composition: One person in cosmic emptiness — absolute solitude. Earth as background blue sphere — beautiful but unreachable.
- Lighting/Color: Space's extreme lighting — direct sunlight and complete darknessalternating. Earth-reflected blue light.
- Editing rhythm: Everything completed within long takes — disaster occurrence, character reaction, survival process all in one uninterrupted shot.
- Sound design: Space's silence — soundconducted through character body contact, not through air. Breathing is the onlypermanent sound.
- Core execution principle: Survival is pure — stripped of all social attributes, what is a person facing death? Long takes' real-time sensation — when the camera doesn't cut, audience and character are in the same time. Rebirth metaphor — character emerging from space capsulelikefrom a womb emerging baby.

**Spielberg Methodology (Disaster dimension)**
- Shot language: Experiencing disaster from character perspective — camera is notdistant watching disaster but with character inside disaster. Handheld chaos. Reaction shots — fear on character's face is more powerful than disaster itself.
- Composition: Character facing incomprehensible thing — looking up from below at immense existence. Child's perspective —the low angle makes everything larger and more terrifying.
- Lighting/Color: Varies by film, but signature backlit flashlight beam — in darkness, a beam of light hits something that shouldn't be there.
- Editing rhythm: Compact — disaster sections highly edited, high information density. But key emotional moments slow down — slow motion of a father holding child's hand running.
- Sound design: John Williams orchestral music — complete emotional spectrum from terror to magnificent. Sound design's information function — audience first "hears" disaster (footsteps, vibration sounds, alarms) then "sees" it.
- Core execution principle: Personal perspective disaster — experiencing global catastrophe through one family's experience. Wonder and fear coexist — disaster also holds beauty and theinconceivable. Children's presence — adds a layer of "protect the innocent" urgency to disaster.
