## Action & Conflict Library

### C1. Modern Action
**Core challenge**: Clarity of action, spatial choreography, and physical realism of impact.

**Stahelski Methodology**
- **Shot language**: Wide shots showing full-body action — not relying on fast cuts to conceal; let the audience see complete body movement. Multi-opponent combat in long takes — the camera darts through the combat but remains uncut; action choreography and execution happen in continuous time and space.
- **Composition**: Space used as a combat tool — stairs, corridors, library bookshelves all participate in combat. Every environment has unique spatial rules; combat style changes with the space.
- **Lighting/Color**: Neon colors — blue, red, and purple extreme colors form the visual identity. Night scenes primarily; city lights are the primary light source.
- **Editing rhythm**: Minimal cuts within action sections — one long take can cover combat with multiple opponents. Each strike's impact is conveyed through real reaction time and sound, not fast cuts.
- **Sound design**: Strike sounds' bone feel — every punch, every throw has weight. Gunshots at real volume levels. Electronic music score.
- **Core execution principle**: Action clarity — the audience must be able to understand what happened. Physical authenticity — characters get tired, injured, bleed. World-building conveyed through action — different factions have different combat styles.

**Woo Methodology**
- **Shot language**: Slow motion is core — the hero's key actions are magnified by slow motion into visual poetry. Multi-angle coverage — the same action shot from three or four angles then cut together. The camera does an arcing movement around characters, weaving through flying doves and shell casings.
- **Composition**: Dual-gun symmetry — the character's dual-wielding posture is a signature composition. Doves, candles, and crucifixes as religious imagery. Confrontation composition — two people facing each other; space permeated by smoke.
- **Lighting/Color**: Backlit hero silhouettes — characters standing before light sources are outlined. Light beams in smoke. Warm tones for brotherhood sections; cool tones for confrontation sections.
- **Editing rhythm**: Slow motion and normal speed alternating — rhythm like music's strong and weak beats. Normal-speed chaotic combat suddenly cuts into a slow-motion "hero moment."
- **Sound design**: Gunshots stylized — not pursuing realism but rhythm. The score is emotionally heightened. Bullets pass through the sound of space.
- **Core execution principle**: Violence IS aesthetics — action is not just a narrative function but independent visual poetry. Brotherhood — respect between opponents matters more than victory or defeat. Ritual — the final duel is like a religious ceremony.

**Miller Methodology**
- **Shot language**: Frame center rule — key visual information is always at the frame center, because at high speed the audience's eyes can only stare at the center. This rule allows the audience to still comprehend at extreme cutting speeds.
- **Composition**: Motion vectors — movement direction within the frame is precisely controlled; audience attention follows movement direction. Foreground flying dust and debris increase the sensation of speed.
- **Lighting/Color**: Desert orange-yellow and sky blue — a high-contrast dual-color system. Metal reflections and flame orange light.
- **Editing rhythm**: Extremely fast but readable — each shot only conveys one unit of information; fast cutting only accelerates information transmission speed, not to confuse information. Rhythm like percussion — has strong beats (explosions, collisions) and weak beats (character reactions).
- **Sound design**: Engine sounds are the main melody — different vehicles have different sound "personalities." Metal collisions, explosions, and wind form a symphony.
- **Core execution principle**: Visual narrative — dialogue is minimal; all information is transmitted through image and action. Practical shooting priority — real vehicles, real stunts, and real dust have more impact than CG. Chase IS narrative — not "telling a story during a chase" but "the chase itself IS the story."

### C2. Wuxia
**Core challenge**: Poeticization of action, *yijing* (artistic conception / poetic atmosphere) of space, and correspondence between technique and character interior.

**Ang Lee Methodology (Wuxia dimension)**
- **Shot language**: Flying camera — in wire-work action, the camera follows characters flying between rooftops and bamboo forests. Action scenes dance-ified — technique choreography is like ballet. Long takes show continuous action sequences.
- **Composition**: Landscape painting-style scenery — characters battle in waterfalls, bamboo forests, and deserts; nature is the largest set. Vertical space utilization — from ground to rooftop to sky.
- **Lighting/Color**: Natural light's Chinese landscape texture — morning mist, sunset, moonlight. Color tones are subdued and elegant — ink-wash style desaturation.
- **Editing rhythm**: Action sections alternating between flowing long takes and precise fast cuts — a coherent flight sequence followed by sudden precise close-ups.
- **Sound design**: Tan Dun-style East-West fusion score — cello and erhu. Weapon collision metal sounds stylized. Wind and fabric billowing sounds.
- **Core execution principle**: Wuxia's "xia" — combat is not for victory but for a certain conviction. Action IS interior — every technique choice reflects the character's current psychological state. Suppressed emotion released through combat — two people fighting may be more intimate than two people embracing.

**Tsui Hark Methodology**
- **Shot language**: Creativity-priority camera movement — the camera can be tied to a sword, placed under horse hooves, or weaving through flames. Perspective unconstrained by physical limitation. Fast zooms and whip pans increase the chaotic energy feel.
- **Composition**: Extreme spatial utilization — narrow alleys, inn staircases, tavern multi-level structures; combat uses every inch of space. High visual density — multiple things happening simultaneously in the frame.
- **Lighting/Color**: Intense colors — red lanterns, gold Buddha statues, emerald bamboo forests. Extreme light-shadow contrast — sunlight weaving through architectural gaps in strong beams. Smoke and flying sand create volumetric light.
- **Editing rhythm**: Extremely fast — fragmented fast cuts create dazzling speed in chaotic combat. But the key "kill shot" can suddenly slow down, letting one sword strike's weight land.
- **Sound design**: Weapon sounds extremely stylized — sword wind, hidden weapons piercing the air, palm wind. Score intense. Battle cries.
- **Core execution principle**: Imagination is the first weapon — wuxia's "kung fu" is unconstrained by physical law limitations. World-building richness — every detail (menu, street, shop sign) constructs a living *jianghu*. Speed and density — high information volume, fast rhythm, without giving breathing space.

**Hu Jinquan Methodology**
- **Shot language**: Fixed camera + precise character blocking — opera-style blocking; character entrances and exits are like stage entrances and exits. Overhead shots and low-angle shots have ritualism. Long takes in bamboo forests, inns, and mountain paths.
- **Composition**: Beijing opera stage spatial sense — minimalist space; narrative carried by character movement and positioning. Natural landscape as "heaven and earth" presence — humans are minuscule but dignified between heaven and earth.
- **Lighting/Color**: Natural light — sunlight through bamboo leaves, morning mist on mountain paths, sunset at an ancient temple. Color subdued and elegant, approaching ink-wash.
- **Editing rhythm**: Beijing opera-style rhythm — still, still, still, explode. Extended confrontation and waiting followed by an instantaneous eruption. The action itself is extremely short — one strike settles victory or defeat.
- **Sound design**: Gong and drum points — Beijing opera martial-scene percussion rhythm applied in editing. Ambient sound emptiness sense — wind, water, birdsong.
- **Core execution principle**: Wuxia's *liubai* (intentional blank space / artistic omission) — the highest-level combat lies not in the quantity of moves but in one strike's weight. Space IS *yijing* (artistic conception / poetic atmosphere) — the bamboo forest is not just a combat venue but the externalization of a certain spiritual state. The solitude of *xia* — the true *xia* always faces alone.

### C3. War
**Core challenge**: Visual contrast between chaos and order, individual minuscule within collective violence.

**Spielberg Methodology (War dimension)**
- **Shot language**: Handheld chaos — making the audience feel they are on the battlefield. Fast pans following soldiers weaving through bullet rain. Camera stained with mud, water drops, and blood.
- **Composition**: Momentary order within chaos — in complete collapse, one soldier's facial close-up suddenly quiets everything.
- **Lighting/Color**: Desaturated film texture — approaching combat photography realism. Gray-green tones.
- **Editing rhythm**: Battle sections extremely fast and chaotic — the audience, like soldiers, cannot process all information. But between battles, it suddenly slows — silence is more terrifying than gunfire.
- **Sound design**: Bullet sound directionality — the audience can hear where bullets come from. Tinnitus after explosions. Soldiers' gasping breath and shouting.
- **Core execution principle**: War is not a heroism stage but a hell of fear. Personal perspective — seeing big history through one small person's eyes. Survival is not victory — the living are not better than the dead, only luckier.

**Nolan Methodology (War dimension)**
- Multi-timeline cross-cutting — land, sea, and air, three lines advancing on different time scales (one week, one day, one hour) simultaneously edited. Time compression creates urgency. IMAX actually shot physical impact — plane engine sounds, waves, shells' somatic vibration.

**Mendes Methodology**
- **Shot language**: "Pseudo-oner" — the entire film is like one uninterrupted long take; the camera follows soldiers passing through the battlefield. Time and space unfold in one continuous experience.
- **Composition**: Trench depth — ahead is unknown death; behind is the path taken. Character always advancing in the frame. Beauty in ruins — bombed churches, rivers lit by flares at night.
- **Lighting/Color**: One day's light changes — from early morning to evening to dawn; natural light changes mark time and emotion. Night scenes only use in-scene firelight and flares.
- **Editing rhythm**: "No cutting" — pseudo-long take achieved through hidden transitions; the audience's time experience synchronizes with the character's time experience.
- **Sound design**: 360-degree ambient sound surround — the audience hears everything the character hears, no more, no less. Sudden gunfire startles audience and character simultaneously.
- **Core execution principle**: Real-time experience — without giving the audience a detachment opportunity. Task simplicity and execution difficulty — "deliver a letter," this simple task becomes an impossible journey on the battlefield. Time IS life — every second, someone is dying.

### C4. Disaster
**Core challenge**: Presentation of scale, individual minuscule before natural forces.

**Emmerich Methodology**
- **Shot language**: Extensive VFX panoramas show disaster scale — cities submerged, buildings collapse, ground split open. Low-angle shots of disaster approaching — making the audience feel a "pressing down" fear.
- **Composition**: Human vs. natural force contrast — humans are extremely small while the disaster is enormous. Iconic landmark destruction — familiar landmarks destroyed to create impact.
- **Lighting/Color**: Grey and dim — dust, smoke, and storm block the sky. Occasional sunlight penetrating — representing hope.
- **Editing rhythm**: Slow setup before disaster, extremely fast during disaster. Multi-line cross-cutting — people in different locations simultaneously facing disaster.
- **Sound design**: Low-frequency roar — earthquake, tsunami, storm's somatic sound. Human screaming swallowed by immense natural sounds.
- **Core execution principle**: Scale staircase — disaster level after level larger. Family is a microcosm of human society — protecting family IS protecting humanity. Disaster's democracy — all classes are on the same boat.

**Cuarón Methodology (Disaster/Survival dimension)**
- **Shot language**: Ultra-long takes (over ten minutes uncut) create real-time immersion. Camera 360-degree rotation in a weightless environment.
- **Composition**: One person in cosmic emptiness — absolute solitude. Earth as a background blue sphere — beautiful but unreachable.
- **Lighting/Color**: Space's extreme lighting — direct sunlight and complete darkness alternating. Earth-reflected blue light.
- **Editing rhythm**: Everything completed within long takes — disaster occurrence, character reaction, survival process all in one uninterrupted shot.
- **Sound design**: Space's silence — sound conducted through character body contact, not through air. Breathing is the only permanent sound.
- **Core execution principle**: Survival is pure — stripped of all social attributes, what is a person facing death? Long takes' real-time sensation — when the camera doesn't cut, audience and character are in the same time. Rebirth metaphor — character emerging from a space capsule is like a baby emerging from a womb.

**Spielberg Methodology (Disaster dimension)**
- **Shot language**: Experiencing disaster from a character's perspective — the camera is not distantly watching the disaster but is with the character inside the disaster. Handheld chaos. Reaction shots — fear on a character's face is more powerful than the disaster itself.
- **Composition**: Character facing an incomprehensible thing — looking up from below at an immense existence. Child's perspective — the low angle makes everything larger and more terrifying.
- **Lighting/Color**: Varies by film, but signature backlit flashlight beam — in darkness, a beam of light hits something that shouldn't be there.
- **Editing rhythm**: Compact — disaster sections highly edited, high information density. But key emotional moments slow down — slow motion of a father holding a child's hand while running.
- **Sound design**: John Williams orchestral music — the complete emotional spectrum from terror to the magnificent. Sound design's information function — the audience first "hears" the disaster (footsteps, vibration sounds, alarms) then "sees" it.
- **Core execution principle**: Personal perspective disaster — experiencing global catastrophe through one family's experience. Wonder and fear coexist — disaster also holds beauty and the inconceivable. Children's presence — adds a layer of "protect the innocent" urgency to disaster.
