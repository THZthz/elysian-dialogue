## Ultra-Short Structure Types (What-If)

### Structure A: Classic Three-Part (Concept Presentation → Progression → Reversal)
Most universal structure, suitable for most What-If concepts.
- **Concept Presentation (first 15-30s)**: One image or action makes the audience instantly understand the rule. No explanation, directly show consequences. Best as a visual suspense that makes them go "huh?"
- **Progression (middle 30-90s)**: Show 2-5 slices of the concept's consequences, layer by layer progressive. Each slice extremely short (5-15s), using visual density to create impact. Slices must have a progressive relationship — each one more extreme, more absurd, or more quotidian than the last.
- **Reversal (final 15-30s)**: One image or sentence that completely subverts the previously established understanding. The reversal must be the concept's own logical extension, not a forced twist.

### Structure B: Progressive Collapse
Suitable for "rules continuously escalate until collapse" concepts.
- **Rule Establishment (~20s)**: Show the rule's basic form — looks pretty reasonable.
- **First Escalation (~20s)**: Rule becomes stricter / more absurd, but people adapt.
- **Second Escalation (~20s)**: Rule escalates to absurd levels, but people still adapt.
- **Collapse Point (~20s)**: Rule pushed to logical extreme limit; the entire system collapses in an instant. OR — everyone has completely adapted to the most absurd state; what collapses is not the rule but the audience's cognition.
- **Afterimage (final 10s)**: A quiet image after collapse — daily life in ruins.

### Structure C: Dual-Line Juxtaposition
Suitable for "reversal juxtaposition" combination method concepts.
Simultaneously show two lines — Line A (seeming normal/warm/beautiful thing) and Line B (also seeming normal/warm/beautiful). Two lines advance visually in parallel (split screen, cross-cutting, or same-frame foreground/background). At some moment, the audience suddenly realizes the relationship between the two lines — A and B are actually two sides of the same thing, or A's beauty is built on B's cruelty. This "realizing" moment IS the reversal. No need for characters to say it.

### Structure D: Loop
Suitable for "inescapable predicament" or "history repeats" concepts.
One complete loop: Starting point (State A) → development → seeming resolution → back to State A. Key: the second time seeing State A, the audience's understanding is already different. First time: this is a normal beginning. Second time: this is an inescapable cage. Can cut to black as the second loop begins — letting the audience themselves realize "here we go again."

### Structure E: Reverse-Reveal
Suitable for "the truth was always in front of you but the audience didn't see it" concepts.
First show the result — a strange, incomprehensible image. Then reverse-engineer the causes — step by step reveal how this image came to be. Each step of revelation makes the image more reasonable — but also more horrifying / absurd / ironic. The final step of revelation makes the audience understand everything — then re-see the first image; its meaning is completely different.
