## Audio-Visual Language Arsenal

Short-form works are the legitimate space for audio-visual technical display. Below are selectable audio-visual techniques — can be used singly or in combination.

### Camera Language
- **Oner (Long Take)**: No editing from start to finish; one continuous shot through the entire work. Effect: realism, immersion, time's irreversibility. Suitable for: concepts needing "presence" or needing the audience to experience time's passage alongside the character.
- **Fixed-Frame Timelapse**: Camera completely motionless; time accelerates through the frame. Effect: contrast between what changes and what doesn't — only the perspective is unchanging; everything else changes. Suitable for: concepts spanning large time scales.
- **Continuous Dolly**: The camera continues at constant-speed movement, passing through space. Effect: spatial revelation — the audience following the camera movement continuously discovers new information. Suitable for: concepts where space itself carries information.
- **First-Person POV**: The entire work is some character/object's subjective perspective. Effect: extreme sense of immersion; restricted information forces the audience to only see what the character sees. Suitable for: perspective-substitution How-to-Tell or What-If concepts needing audience "presence."
- **Macro / Microscopic**: Shooting scales invisible to the naked eye. Effect: familiar things become strange — a drop of water becomes an ocean; skin becomes terrain. Suitable for: scale-jumping How-to-Tell.

### Editing / Time
- **Jump Cut Montage**: Rapid time jumps within the same frame — character's posture, clothing, state quickly switching under the same camera position. Effect: time compression, repetition's absurdity. Suitable for: showing "day after day" concepts or rapidly extrapolating rule consequences.
- **Split Screen**: Frame divided into 2-4 zones to simultaneously show different content. Effect: contrast, juxtaposition, parallel timelines. Suitable for: dual-line juxtaposition structure, reversal juxtaposition combination.
- **Cross Cutting**: Rapid switching between two or more lines. Effect: contrast acceleration, tension accumulation, hints at the relationship between lines. Suitable for: two seemingly unrelated things ultimately revealed as connected.
- **Reverse Playback**: Image playing in reverse. Effect: familiar actions becoming strange; result appearing before cause. Suitable for: time-surgery How-to-Tell, loop structures.
- **Freeze Frame / Time Freeze**: Image frozen at a key moment. Effect: forces the audience to notice a certain detail; creates suspense or punctuation. Suitable for: emphasizing a reversal moment within dense visual information flow.

### Composition
- **Symmetry**: Image strictly left-right / top-bottom symmetrical. Effect: sense of order, ritual, unease (overly perfect order hints at a certain oppression). Suitable for: showing institutionalized, rule-based concepts.
- **Foreground/Background Narrative**: Foreground doing one thing; background happens another; the two mutually annotate. Effect: juxtaposition without editing — the audience discovers two layers of information in the same frame. Suitable for: concepts needing juxtaposition without split screen.
- **Color Restriction**: Entire work uses only limited colors (even just black-and-white + one color). Effect: visual "rule sensation" — color itself becomes a narrative element. Suitable for: color having a direct connection to the concept.

### Sound
- **Picture-Sound Misalignment**: Image shows A; sound belongs to B. Effect: collision of two information layers — the audience simultaneously receives two different signals. Suitable for: irony, contrast.
- **Ambient Sound Amplification**: Everyday ambient sounds amplified to abnormal volume. Effect: familiar becomes uneasy; the daily becomes abnormal. Suitable for: concepts needing "terror within the everyday."
- **Silence**: Completely no sound at key moments. Effect: forces the audience to concentrate attention on the image; creates a vacuum sensation. Suitable for: sudden silence after high-density information flow — silence itself IS the climax.

### Interface / Media
- **Screen Recording**: Entire work is a phone/computer screen recording. Effect: extreme contemporary feel; the audience feels like they're prying into someone else's phone. Suitable for: form-simulation How-to-Tell.
- **Mockumentary / Pseudo-Documentary**: Pretending to be a real documentary, news report, or educational video. Effect: wrapping an absurd core in an authentic shell — the "serious" form makes absurdity more absurd. Suitable for: What-If concepts needing a "serious" shell as *fanchen* (contrast by opposition).
- **Data Visualization**: Telling stories through the visual language of charts, data, and statistics. Effect: detached, objective numbers — behind them lies immense personal tragedy. The calmer the numbers, the more shocking the content. Suitable for: concepts needing an "objective feel" to create contrast.

### Selection Principles
1. **Form serves content.** First determine the concept, then choose the AV technique. Not "I want to use split screen" then finding a concept to match.
2. **Focus on 1-2 core techniques per work.** Don't stuff five AV techniques into 3 minutes — that's a technical display compilation, not a work. Choose the 1-2 techniques that most strongly reinforce the concept and use them to the extreme.
3. **AV techniques need progression.** Can't use one technique unchanged from beginning to end — even with a "fixed camera," the content within the frame must progress. Techniques can escalate, mutate, or break at the ending.
4. **Technique itself can be the reversal tool.** The highest-level usage: the reversal is not just at the content level; the AV form itself also reverses.
