## Theme & Relationship Library

### D1. Love
**Core challenge**: Visualizing chemistry between two people, use of distance and time.

**Wong Kar-wai (Love dimension)**: Time, distance, missed encounters. Characters' physical distance precisely corresponds to psychological distance. The more enclosed the space, the more concentrated the emotion. "If memory came in cans" — time's irreversibility and love's expiration date are eternal themes.

**Linklater Methodology**
- Shot language: Handheld following two people walking and talking in a city — the camera is a third wanderer. Mid-shots and medium close-ups maintain both people's spatial relationship visibly. No fancy camera work — realism is everything.
- Composition: Two-person walking composition — two people alongside or face to face in frame; city streets flow in background. Cafes, trains, park benches — dialogue spaces are intimate but public.
- Lighting/Color: Natural light — European cities' golden afternoon light, nighttime street lamps. Real light without embellishment.
- Editing rhythm: Complete dialogue in long takes — let dialogue develop naturally; don't forcibly create drama. Editing follows dialogue's rhythm — don't cut away during pauses.
- Sound design: City ambient sound — trams, street music, distant church bells. Minimal score — dialogue itself IS music.
- Core execution principle: Dialogue IS intimacy — two people talking deeper and deeper IS the process of falling in love. Time's cruelty — sunrise/sunset as deadline; the sense of urgency of time passing makes every sentence precious. Imperfection IS authenticity — characters say wrong things, feel awkward, don't know whether to kiss.

**Iwai Shunji (Love dimension)**: Love is only seen after it's lost. Written words (handwritten letters, messages) are visualized emotional acts. Seasons are emotional calendars — winter's cold preserves the temperature of a certain emotional attachment.

### D2. Family / Kinship
**Core challenge**: Emotional density within daily details, spatialization of power relations, the failure and attempt of cross-generational communication.

**Kore-eda (Family dimension)**: The dining table is the map of family power relations — who sits where, who moves first, who is absent. Children are the best "mirrors" — their reactions to the adult world reveal truths adults themselves cannot see. Blood ≠ family — "eating together" defines family more than "bleeding together."

**Ang Lee Methodology (Family dimension)**
- Shot language: Classical steady camera — slow dolly and precise composition. No handheld. Camera like a cultivated observer — maintaining polite distance.
- Composition: Dining table composition — Chinese family power relations presented through seating arrangement and food passing. Door frames and window frames — family members "trapped" within family structure.
- Lighting/Color: Warm but not intense — home space's light like memory's home.
- Editing rhythm: Medium — unhurried; let family's daily rhythm naturally present itself. But emotionally eruptive confrontation scenes use tighter shot-reverse-shot.
- Sound design: Cooking sounds — chopping vegetables, stir-frying, steam. These sounds are the family's "heartbeat." Score understated.
- Core execution principle: Suppressed volcano — beneath the surface calm of family is decades of accumulated unsaid words. Cooking IS love — food is the primary way Chinese families express emotion. Two generations' dialogue is not in words but in actions — a father giving his daughter an extra chopstick of food carries more weight than saying "I love you."

**Ken Loach Methodology**
- Shot language: Documentary handheld — following a character's daily life like filming a documentary. No strict camera positions — camera is like a person present, finding the best observation angle as events happen.
- Composition: Not pursuing image beauty — pursuing truth. Working-class family spaces: small apartments, social housing, corner pubs.
- Lighting/Color: Completely natural light — no fill, no color grading. Northern England's gray skies.
- Editing rhythm: Following life's rhythm — no acceleration or deceleration. Complete dialogue and events.
- Sound design: Real ambient sound. Dialects and accents are important components of character identity.
- Core execution principle: Poverty is not an abstract concept but concrete daily difficulties — can't pay the electric bill, can't afford school uniform. Dignity — even in the most difficult circumstances, people strive to maintain dignity. Humor — the harder the life, the more laughter is needed; working-class humor is the sharpest.

### D3. Youth / Coming-of-Age
**Core challenge**: Authenticity and weight of character feelings — "feeling that your own feelings are the most important thing in the world."

**Gerwig Methodology**
- Shot language: Active handheld — energy-filled but not chaotic. Following characters running, jumping, laughing. Medium close-ups maintain intimacy.
- Composition: Character's body language occupies frame — posture lying on bed, walking posture, arguing gestures. Interior spaces' lived-in feel — messy bedroom, notes on fridge.
- Lighting/Color: Warm California light — golden afternoon light. Color tones like memory's youth — slightly overly warm, because recollection always comes with its own filter.
- Editing rhythm: Dialogue-driven but not dragging — characters speak fast with high density. Montage shows fragments of daily life.
- Sound design: Pop music marking era — each song corresponding to an emotional stage. Character voice is core — speed, pitch, pauses.
- Core execution principle: Youth's essence is "feeling that your own feelings are the most important thing in the world" — the camera grants the character's emotions complete respect. Hometown love-hate — the place you want to escape is precisely the place that shaped you. Relationships between women — the complexity between mothers, daughters, female friends is richer than romance.

**Yang Dechang Methodology**
- Shot language: Fixed camera or extremely slow dolly. Long takes let scenes develop completely. No close-ups for emphasis — audience finds focal points themselves in the frame. Human minuscule sense in extreme wide shots.
- Composition: Taipei's urban spaces — apartment building corridors, classrooms, streets. Glass and mirror reflections — what the character sees and what the audience sees may differ. Multi-layer space — foreground, midground, background simultaneously have something happening.
- Lighting/Color: Natural light — fluorescent cold white light, Taipei's gray skies. Unadorned real light.
- Editing rhythm: Slow — each scene has ample breathing time. Long dialogue uninterrupted.
- Sound design: City noise — motorcycles, TVs, distant construction. Minimal score.
- Core execution principle: Growing up is a process of "discovering the world is not what you thought it was" — from a child/teenager's perspective, the world's hypocrisy and complexity are revealed bit by bit. Multi-line narrative — growing up is not just the protagonist's affair; everyone is struggling in their own life. Film IS education — not lecturing, but "seeing."

**Linklater (Youth dimension)**: Time itself IS narrative — 12 years to make one film; time on screen is real. Growing up is not an "event" but a "process" — no clear turning point; change is accumulated. Daily fragments — a haircut, a song, a move; these are not plot but time's markers.

### D4. Biography
**Core challenge**: Visual reconstruction of real people, compression of timespan, balance between dissection and tribute.

**Scorsese (Biography dimension)**: Timespan processing — using voiceover and montage to compress years. Character speaks directly to camera — "this is my story" self-awareness. Freeze frame marking key life node. Music montage is the Swiss Army knife of biopics — one song + one montage can span across a decade.

**Fincher (Biography dimension)**: Biography is not tribute but dissection — coldly show one person's talent and flaws. Dialogue density and speed reflect a character's thinking speed. Investigation-style narrative structure — like dissecting one person's entire life.

**Spike Lee Methodology**
- Shot language: Signature "double dolly" — character stands on a dolly being pushed toward camera; background moves but distance between character and camera remains unchanged — character like floating. Direct address to camera monologues. Dutch angles at tense moments.
- Composition: Symmetrical frontal portraiture — character stares directly at audience. Street and community environmental composition — characters and the spaces where they live are inseparable.
- Lighting/Color: High saturation — intense colors reflect intense emotions. Not avoiding brightness — the beauty of dark skin in good light.
- Editing rhythm: Music-driven montage. News footage and narrative cross-cutting.
- Sound design: Jazz, hip-hop, soul — music is cultural identity made audible.
- Core execution principle: The personal IS the political — one person's story IS one community's, one race's, one era's story. Anger is legitimate — the film does not conceal anger or apologize for it. Freedom of form — can blend narrative film, documentary, musical, and direct-to-audience speech within the same film.

### D5. Sports / Competition
**Core challenge**: Visual tension of competition, body as narrative carrier, humanity beyond winning and losing.

**Russell Methodology**
- Shot language: Handheld energy — camera follows character's movement; sweat and gasping breath palpable. Competition scenes multi-angle coverage — from athlete's perspective, opponent's perspective, audience perspective.
- Composition: Boxing ring / arena's enclosed space — character trapped by ropes / boundaries. Corner coach and family — supporters at frame edges.
- Sound design: Venue reverberation — audience shouts echoing in enclosed space. Strike sounds and breathing.
- Core execution principle: Competition is internal war externalized — what the character fights in the ring is not the opponent but their own demons. Family is harder than competition — problems faced at home are more thorny than opponents in the arena.

**Boyle Methodology (Competition dimension)**
- Shot language: Extreme cinematography — underwater photography, macro photography, cameras fixed to sports equipment. Fast zooms and whip pans.
- Composition: Body in motion — muscle contraction, water splashing, sweat droplet trajectory. Extreme close-up sensory impact.
- Lighting/Color: High saturation — blue pool, red track, green field. Color pushed to the extreme.
- Editing rhythm: Extremely fast montage — fragments of training, competition, memory intersecting. Music drives editing rhythm.
- Sound design: Electronic and techno — movement rhythm synchronized with music BPM. Body sounds — heartbeat, breathing, skeletal stress.
- Core execution principle: Sport is visual poetry — not just recording competition but transforming sport into pure visual experience. The character's body IS the protagonist — every muscle, every drop of sweat has drama. Time manipulation — key seconds can be stretched into minutes.

**Miller Methodology**
- Shot language: Restrained — no fancy camera work; let performance support everything. Mid-shots and medium close-ups primarily. Competition scene compositions maintain observer's distance.
- Composition: Clean — background clean; focus on character faces. Locker room, training field, office — sport's "backstage" is more important than the arena.
- Lighting/Color: Desaturated realistic tones — not romanticizing sport. Fluorescent-lit locker rooms, overcast training fields.
- Editing rhythm: Slow — let character's thinking and decision-making have time to unfold. Competition scenes not pursuing fast-cut thrill.
- Sound design: Ambient sound primarily — locker room echoes, arena noise. Minimal score.
- Core execution principle: Sport IS a system — not just individual triumph but system operation. Data and intuition — tension between rational analysis and human intuition. Failure is more dramatic than success — an athlete's valley reveals more humanity than their highlight reel.

### D6. Tragedy (as Emotional Narrative)
**Core challenge**: How to build deep empathy without sliding into emotional manipulation, how to give pain weight without making it performative.

**Lee Chang-dong (Emotional Narrative dimension)**: Character dignity — even in the most miserable circumstances, characters do not lose their complexity as human beings. Without giving the audience "cry-command making" — don't use score to underscore, don't use close-ups for tear-jerking; a character's pain is conveyed through behavior, not expression. Audience empathy comes from "recognition" — a character does an action the audience would do in the same situation; empathy happens at that moment.

**Farhadi (Emotional Narrative dimension)**: Tragedy needs no villain — the irreconcilable between two good people IS the most genuine tragedy. Moral dilemma physicalized — when a character faces a choice, the choice is executed through a concrete physical action (signing, opening a door, dialing a phone). Information absence is cruel — if the character had known the truth one step earlier, tragedy wouldn't have happened.

**Aronofsky (Emotional Narrative dimension)**: Obsession as tragedy's engine — the character's extreme pursuit of something (perfect performance, the final match, eternal youth) is itself the self-destruct procedure. Audience empathy paradox — the audience understands why the character can't stop, but also knows not stopping means the end. The body's narrative — bodily decline is the visible form of spiritual struggle; it is also the most inescapable tragic dimension.
