## Six-Dimension Tone-Setting Method

### Core Problem per Dimension

| Dimension                | Core Question                                                                    |
|--------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| A. Emotional Mood        | What is the work's emotional temperature and aesthetic posture?                  |
| B. Genre Conventions     | What genre conventions does it follow? Where does it break them?                 |
| C. Action & Conflict     | How does physical confrontation generate impact and narrative meaning?           |
| D. Theme & Relationships | How does audiovisual language present interpersonal relationships and emotion?   |
| E. Narrative Form        | What special requirements does the narrative form itself impose on audiovisuals? |
| F. Social Perspective    | How does the work relate to reality / society / history?                         |

Not every dimension needs a choice. A project typically involves 2-4 dimensions. For example:
- A black comedy road movie → A7 Black Humor (Coen Bros) + E2 Road Movie (Payne) + D1 Love (Linklater's dialogue approach)
- A sci-fi tragedy → B7 Sci-Fi (Villeneuve) + A5 Tragedy (Lee Chang-dong's daily erosion)
- A wuxia mainstream piece → C2 Wuxia (Hu Jinquan) + F4 Mainstream (Chinese jingpin (premium-quality) direction)

From each relevant dimension, borrow 1-2 directors' concrete methodologies. When fusing, establish one "dominant director reference" and several "local borrowings."

### Guided Questioning Strategy

**Never present the dimension menu or director list to the user directly.** Use guided questioning to progressively lock in each dimension:

**Round 1: Emotional and genre direction**
"What do you most want the audience to feel after experiencing this work?"
"If you could describe this work's 'temperament' in one word, what would you choose?"
→ Based on the response, lock in A (emotional mood) and B (genre) direction.

**Round 2: Audiovisual style preferences**
"Is there a film whose 'visual feeling' matches what you want? Doesn't have to be the same genre."
"What do you definitely NOT want? Too flashy camerawork? Too slow rhythm? Too dark imagery?"
→ Based on reference works or exclusion items, precisely locate specific directors.

**Round 3: Theme and form special requirements**
"Does this story have any special narrative form? Multi-timeline, mockumentary, road structure?"
"What is the core relationship? Family, love, adversarial?"
→ Lock in D (theme) and E (form) dimensions.

**Round 4: Social and historical dimension (if applicable)**
"What is this story's relationship to reality? Needs realism or can be stylized?"
→ Lock in F dimension.

### Tone-Setting Output Format
After confirmation, output the director tone-setting:
- **Audiovisual Posture**: The camera as [X], maintaining [X] distance from the character
- **Dimension References**: Emotional mood (core reference: [director]'s [concrete method]), genre direction, theme/relationship, [other relevant dimensions]
- **Fusion Plan**: Overall tone base (underlying tonal foundation) + which sections borrow from which directors
- **Shot Language**: Fixed/handheld/mixed + long take/fast cut/mixed primarily
- **Composition Direction**: Symmetrical/asymmetrical/natural + liubai (intentional blank space / artistic omission)/dense
- **Color & Lighting**: Warm/cool/desaturated + natural light/dramatic light/practical sources
- **Editing Rhythm**: Fast/slow/varied + linear/nonlinear
- **Sound Design**: Score driven/supportive/minimal, ambient sound realistic/stylized
- **Narrative Perspective**: Omniscient/restricted/multi-perspective + audience info greater/equal/less than character
- **Image System**: Visual motifs, imagery direction, visual elements to emphasize
