## Genre Conventions Library

This library translates film-based genre craft into techniques the GM can execute through NARRATOR descriptions, NPC dialogue, and world-state manipulation. Where the original refers to camera, lighting, or editing, the GM uses deliberate narrative focus — what the NARRATOR chooses to describe, at what level of detail, and in what order — to create the same psychological effect.

---

### B1. Thriller
**Core challenge**: Information control and tension construction. What the player knows, when they know it, and through whose perspective — this determines the quality of fear.

**Hitchcock Methodology**
- **Narrative description style**: Every detail the NARRATOR reports is a unit of information. The NARRATOR can linger on a detail that the character does not notice — the player knows something is in the room but the character acts oblivious. The description can systematically adopt a single character's sensory perspective, forcing the player to see only what that character sees.
- **Spatial composition**: Describe a character's line of sight — what they are looking at, what they fail to see. Describe foreground objects that partially block the view of something ominous (a figure half-hidden behind a door, a shape behind frosted glass). The architecture of stairs, windows, and doorways can be described to create a sense of entrapment.
- **Light and color**: Describe light and shadow with precision — what is lit clearly and what is swallowed by darkness. Backlit silhouettes: describe a figure whose face is invisible, standing against a bright window or headlights. Harsh shadows across a room suggest places where danger could crouch.
- **Pacing and rhythm**: The difference between suspense and surprise: for suspense, the NARRATOR describes the threat in advance — the player knows the bomb is under the table and dreads the moment of detonation; for surprise, the threat erupts without warning, described suddenly in terse, fast narration. Violence can be conveyed through fragmentary, brief descriptions that never "show" the full act — only its aftermath.
- **Narrative perspective**: The player knows more than the character — the NARRATOR reveals the bomb under the table while the character chats obliviously. This information asymmetry is the engine of suspense. Occasionally, the NARRATOR can let the player suddenly lose their information advantage, creating disorientation.
- **Environmental sound and sensory texture**: Describe sharp, punctuating sounds — a knife scraping, a sudden shriek. Strategic silence — describe a moment where all ambient sound seems to drop away, leaving only a held breath. Everyday sounds can be described as unsettling (a bird call that sounds too human, a child's laughter at the wrong moment).
- **Core execution principle**: Fear comes from "waiting" rather than "happening" — the explosion is not scary; knowing it will come but not when is terrifying. Ordinary characters in extraordinary circumstances — the protagonist is not a hero but an ordinary person drawn into danger they cannot comprehend. MacGuffin — what drives the plot is unimportant; what matters is the humanity characters expose while chasing it.

**Villeneuve Methodology**
- **Narrative description style**: Alternate between vast, sweeping descriptions of scale (the character is a speck against an immense landscape) and intimate, granular detail (a twitch at the corner of the mouth). The NARRATOR can slowly close distance on a character, describing them from afar then gradually moving in to reveal micro-expressions. Use selective detail — describe only the character sharply, leaving the periphery blurred or unremarked, isolating them.
- **Spatial composition**: Describe spaces that feel perfectly ordered yet profoundly wrong — a symmetrical room where something subtle is out of place. Oppressive spatial detail: low ceilings, corridors that narrow, landscapes smothered in fog. Characters can be described as tiny within the frame of the environment — dwarfed by architecture or terrain.
- **Light and color**: Describe light as natural and motivated yet emotionally charged — pale dawn light that feels accusatory, the orange haze of a dust storm. Use a restrained, desaturated palette of words: ochre, grey-blue, faded green. Fog, smoke, and dust can be described as limiting visibility, softening edges, concealing threats.
- **Pacing and rhythm**: Slow, lingering descriptions — the NARRATOR dwells on a detail longer than seems necessary, building a low thrum of unease. Acceleration is rare and therefore devastating — a sudden burst of terse, rapid narration after long stretches of calm.
- **Narrative perspective**: Tightly locked to the protagonist's awareness — the player only knows what the character perceives. This means the NARRATOR describes only what the character can see, hear, and feel, leaving a constant offscreen threat of the unknown.
- **Environmental sound and sensory texture**: Describe low, somatic sounds — a hum so deep it is felt rather than heard, the vibration of distant machinery. The weight of silence is immense — describe a held quiet, then a sudden, sharp noise. Ambient sound is rendered with painstaking detail: grit scratching underfoot, the drip of water, an engine's far-off drone.
- **Core execution principle**: Sense of scale — the story's backdrop can be cosmic, but fear is always personal and intimate. Slow rhythm is not absence of tension but "slowly tightening" — a hand gradually closing. The enemy can be invisible — the greatest fear comes from not knowing what you are facing.

**Park Chan-wook Methodology (Thriller dimension)**
- **Narrative description style**: Violence can be described in unbroken, lingering detail — not a flurry of blows but a single, protracted action where every second of physical cost and pain is felt. The NARRATOR can focus on minute physiological reactions — a bead of sweat, a trembling hand, blood welling from a cut.
- **Spatial composition**: Confine violence within cramped, enclosed spaces — a prison cell, a narrow corridor, an elevator. Describe how the walls press in, how there is no room to dodge or flee. Use mirrored composition in revenge narratives — describe the avenger in a position that exactly mirrors the original victim's posture, the same space, the same angle of light.
- **Light and color**: Use extreme, saturated color descriptions — jade green tiles, deep crimson walls, ink-blue shadows. Strong contrasts of light and dark, described unflinchingly but not crudely.
- **Pacing and rhythm**: Hold on a moment long past comfort — the NARRATOR describes a still scene, a held gaze, then erupts into rapid, fragmentary narration at the moment of violence. Perspective reversal: the first half of the story is described from one character's viewpoint; the second half revisits the same events from another's, revealing that everything understood was incomplete.
- **Narrative perspective**: Restricted perspective plus delayed reversal — the player experiences the first half through Character A's senses, then the second half re-experiences the same timeline through Character B's. The story peels open like an onion, each layer of truth revealed by whose eyes the player is looking through.
- **Core execution principle**: Revenge is not liberation but another form of imprisonment — after completing revenge, describe the character's space as no more open than before, perhaps even more confined. Violence has consequences — every blow leaves real marks, and subsequent descriptions should reference those injuries. The revelation of secrets is not climax but collapse — truth does not bring relief, only further ruin.

### B2. Psychological Thriller
**Core challenge**: Space is the externalization of psychology; the boundary between reality and hallucination blurs.

**Kubrick Methodology**
- **Narrative description style**: The NARRATOR can follow a character through space in a steady, unbroken flow of description — an inescapable, observing presence. Describe corridors or roads that seem to stretch to a vanishing point, perfectly symmetrical and infinite. Extremely slow, deliberate description of a single advancing detail — like a threat approaching inch by inch.
- **Spatial composition**: Describe spaces with obsessive symmetry — the vanishing point at the exact center of the described scene, everything balanced on either side. This symmetry can feel like a trap, a "sucking in" sensation. Characters can be described staring directly at the player (within the text: "She looks at you from beneath her brows, head lowered, eyes unwavering").
- **Light and color**: Describe light as practical and motivated — the cold white of fluorescent tubes, the harsh cone of a single bare bulb — but push its emotional charge. Cold whites and sterile tones dominate, creating a clinical, inhuman atmosphere.
- **Pacing and rhythm**: Slow and precise — each descriptive beat seems calculated. Alternate between long, unbroken descriptive passages and sudden bursts of terse, clipped narration. Key sequences can be driven by a rhythm of repeated sensory details, like a musical pulse.
- **Narrative perspective**: Cold omniscience — the NARRATOR describes the character's madness with perfect, detached calm. When the character unravels, the prose remains eerily composed; this contrast is the source of fear.
- **Environmental sound and sensory texture**: Describe dissonant sounds — a high, keening note that seems to come from nowhere, a mechanical rhythm that feels like a malfunctioning heart. The soundscape should feel "wrong" in ways that mirror the character's fractured mind.
- **Core execution principle**: Space is the externalization of psychology — describe maze-like hallways for a maze-like mind. Asymmetrical details appearing in otherwise symmetrical spaces signal irrational cracks in a rational world. Repetition is the visual signature of madness — describe the same action (typing, pacing, walking a corridor) again and again, each time with a subtle, accumulating wrongness.

**Aronofsky Methodology (Psychological Thriller dimension)**
- Mirrors and reflections can be described as behaving independently — a character's reflection does not match their actions. What the player perceives through a character's senses may be hallucination; the NARRATOR can describe something that later turns out not to be there. Body horror: describe movement beneath the skin, nails splitting, muscles twitching beyond control — the body betraying its owner. The rhythm of description can accelerate in spiraling cycles, each cycle faster and denser, sweeping character and player into an unstoppable vortex.

**Villeneuve Methodology (Psychological Thriller dimension)**
- The character's face is the primary landscape — describe micro-expressions, a flicker of the eyelid, the tightening of a jaw, as the entire event. Dialogue scenes can be described with oppressive spatial detail: two people across a table, the NARRATOR noting how the space between them seems to shrink, the walls closing in. When the character's psychological state shifts, describe the ambient sensory texture changing with it — the hum of lights growing louder, the air growing heavier.

### B3. Horror
**Core challenge**: Fear rhythm (promise-delay-payoff) and the presentation of the unknown.

**James Wan Methodology**
- **Narrative description style**: First, establish a space with calm, thorough description — let the player know the room's layout, the position of every door and window. Then, describe the character looking one way while the NARRATOR directs attention elsewhere — behind the character, the other side of the door, a dark corner where something shifted.
- **Spatial composition**: Use depth of space in description — something terrifying is in the background, blurred by distance or shadow, identifiable only by a wrong silhouette. Describe a shift in focus: the character is in the foreground, but then the NARRATOR's attention slides to the darkness behind, where something has appeared. Threats can be described at the edges of awareness — a shape that slips out of sight, a door that is now ajar.
- **Light and color**: Describe low illumination — vast areas of blackness with only a faint, trembling light source (candle, moonlight, a dying flashlight). Establish a clear boundary between warm-lit safety and cold-shadowed danger — and then describe that boundary being breached.
- **Pacing and rhythm**: Long, slow descriptive passages accumulate dread — the NARRATOR scans the space, and the player anticipates what will be found. True scares should come a beat earlier or later than expected — if the player tenses for a shock, deliver it after they exhale, or not at all. The best fear is the BANG that never comes.
- **Environmental sound and sensory texture**: Describe silence as the most terrifying presence — when all ambient sound drops away, the player knows something is imminent. Low, throbbing hums at the edge of description. Sudden, sharp sounds — a crash, a scream, a slamming door — described in terse, percussive sentences.
- **Core execution principle**: The unseen is more terrifying than the seen — hint at the monster's existence through its effects: a displaced object, a smell, a sound. Fear's rhythm is promise-delay-payoff — tell the player something is there (promise), make them wait (delay), then reveal it (payoff). The safest space is the most terrifying setting — when the place of refuge becomes the place of threat, there is nowhere to hide.

**Eggers Methodology**
- **Narrative description style**: Use static, almost motionless description — the NARRATOR holds on a scene, letting time stretch. The descriptive voice can feel ritualistic, archaic, as if reciting a liturgy. Adopt a deliberate, period-appropriate vocabulary that makes the world feel sealed off from modernity.
- **Spatial composition**: Describe scenes as if they were period paintings — Rembrandt's chiaroscuro, a woodcut's rough lines. Characters are placed at the center of a dark, enclosing frame of environment. Symmetry hints at a supernatural order.
- **Light and color**: Describe candlelight and firelight as the sole illumination — a small, trembling pool of orange warmth surrounded by absolute black. Use desaturated, almost monochrome color language — grey, sepia, the color of old bone. The quality of light itself conveys period authenticity.
- **Pacing and rhythm**: Slow — let terror ferment within static, lingering descriptions. An image can transform from normal to horrifying purely through the player's shifting attention within the described scene — the NARRATOR suddenly notes a face in the shadows that was there all along.
- **Narrative perspective**: Immersive historical experience — the player is not viewing the past but finding themselves within it. The language, the quality of light, the texture of sound all compel acceptance of another era's perceptual mode.
- **Environmental sound and sensory texture**: Period-accurate sounds — wood groaning, wind howling through chinks, the cry of an animal. Music, if described, should come from period instruments. The soundscape should feel hollow, empty of modern density.
- **Core execution principle**: Terror comes from isolation — all connections to the modern world are severed. Nature itself is terror — the forest, the ocean, the wilderness are not backdrop but a force that consumes. The boundary between faith and superstition is deliberately blurred — the NARRATOR never confirms whether the supernatural is real or a projection of the character's fracturing mind.

**Aster Methodology**
- **Narrative description style**: Use extremely slow, deliberate description, or adopt an overhead, omniscient stance — the NARRATOR looks down on characters as a god might watch ants. Daytime horror: describe terrifying events occurring in bright, sunlit, beautiful environments.
- **Spatial composition**: In wide-scale descriptions, characters become small, doll-like components of a larger, indifferent environment. Every detail of a room — a wall hanging, a household object — is part of the narrative. Hide wrong details within ordinary ensemble descriptions: the player must mentally search the scene to find the anomaly.
- **Light and color**: Brightness is the anti-conventional horror strategy. Describe full daylight, vibrant colors — flowers, clothing, ritual decorations — but the more vivid the description, the greater the unease.
- **Pacing and rhythm**: Slow to the point of suffocation — key horror sequences are described without any release or acceleration. Despair and wailing can be described at relentless length, offering the player no jump-out, no fast relief. Fear is a continuous, unbroken presence.
- **Narrative perspective**: Tightly bound to a grieving protagonist — terror is not an external monster but the internal landscape of loss. The player is forced to experience, alongside the character, the full, unsparing process of bereavement.
- **Environmental sound and sensory texture**: Human cries and screams are the core horror sounds — describe the texture of a sob, the ragged edge of a wail. Ambient sound is dissonant: in a world of birdsong and floral fragrance, something is fundamentally, indescribably wrong.
- **Core execution principle**: Family and community are the source of terror — the most intimate relationships become the most threatening. Daytime horror — without darkness, there is nowhere to hide from the horror under sunlight. Grief is the entrance — the character is made vulnerable by loss; evil seeps through the cracks.

### B4. Crime
**Core challenge**: Visualizing moral gray zones, constructing spaces of power.

**Scorsese Methodology**
- **Narrative description style**: The NARRATOR can move with energy through a space — describing a continuous walk from a back kitchen into a noisy dining room, following a character through a club. Key moments can be described in a "freeze-frame" — the action halts on a single, telling detail. Violence or moments of vanity can be described in slow, exaggerated detail.
- **Spatial composition**: Describe power through spatial arrangement — who sits in the center, who stands at the periphery. Bars, restaurants, and clubs are power's stage; describe where each person is positioned as a map of hierarchy. Characters can speak directly to the player through the text, breaking the fourth wall.
- **Light and color**: Describe intense, saturated light — red bar lights, blue night, gold from chandeliers. Neon, headlights, light cutting through smoke. The light is dramatic but always anchored in a real source within the scene.
- **Pacing and rhythm**: High-energy description — rapid, punchy sentences for dialogue and action, with rhythmic, music-like patterns. Then, a sudden deceleration into slow, weighted description when violence occurs, letting its consequence land.
- **Narrative perspective**: First-person voiceover or tight third — the protagonist narrates their own rise and fall, and the player hears their justifications, their boasts, their self-deception.
- **Environmental sound and sensory texture**: Classic rock or pop described as playing in the scene — the song choice itself is commentary. A complete song can be described as driving an entire sequence of action, its rhythm matched by the pace of narration.
- **Core execution principle**: The crime world's appeal — first make the player feel drawn in by its charm and energy, then slowly reveal the cost. Rise and fall — the first half is the thrill of ascent; the second half is the terror of the drop. The character knows the path's endpoint but chooses to keep walking — the tragedy is not in ignorance but in knowing and proceeding anyway.

**Mann Methodology**
- **Narrative description style**: Describe city nights with a documentarian's precision — neon and streetlight are the only illumination; the quality of light is cool and digital. In dialogue and pursuit, the description can feel handheld, intimate, breathless. Widescreen cityscape descriptions establish scale.
- **Spatial composition**: The city at night is the soul — describe the grid of lights, the neon coastline. A character's physical position in the city mirrors their social position. Confrontation scenes are described like western showdowns — two people facing each other in an empty, echoing space.
- **Light and color**: Cool blue city nights — neon, sodium-vapor yellow, LED white. Interiors are lit by practical sources: fluorescent tubes, computer screens. The description can note the particular grain and coolness of the light.
- **Pacing and rhythm**: Action is described in real time — no compression or expansion, just the confusion and noise of a gunfight rendered with immediacy. Dialogue scenes are given breathing room — the respect between two opponents needs time to be felt.
- **Narrative perspective**: Dual protagonist — the NARRATOR follows both cop and criminal with equal attention, granting both equal dignity and presence.
- **Environmental sound and sensory texture**: Gunshots described as deafening, chaotic, unprocessed. The white noise of the city at night. Electronic or post-rock tracks described as underscoring the solitude.
- **Core execution principle**: Professionalism — both cop and criminal are masters of their craft; their confrontation is a duel of skill. Solitude — the two opponents are actually the same kind of person; they understand each other better than their own colleagues or families. The city is a third character — its rhythm, light, and temperature participate in every decision.

**Ritchie Methodology**
- **Narrative description style**: The NARRATOR's voice can be active, performative — rapid cuts between angles, fast acceleration and sudden slow-motion, like visual hiccups rendered in text. Description can feel like a technical display, full of verve and swagger.
- **Spatial composition**: The described scene is compact, crowded — stuffed with people, props, and information. Parallel actions can be described through rapid intercutting or side-by-side juxtaposition. Characters can narrate their own actions to the player in a knowing, meta-textual way.
- **Light and color**: Stylized — yellow-brown London tones or overexposed Mediterranean sunlight. Light quality is matched to the speed of the narration: sharp and crisp for fast sequences, softened for slow-motion moments.
- **Pacing and rhythm**: Extremely fast and nonlinear — flashbacks, flash-forwards, parallel threads all interwoven. When a narrator speaks, the description leaps between times and spaces to illustrate their words. The rhythm is like rap — beat, flow, punchline.
- **Narrative perspective**: Multi-line narrative — multiple characters acting independently, their paths colliding at key moments. Different characters' versions of events contradict each other — the NARRATOR may present one account, then another.
- **Environmental sound and sensory texture**: Rock and electronic music driving the described rhythm. Sound effects described as cartoonish and exaggerated — a punch described like a drum hit, an engine like a bass line. Dialogue is fast, thick with accent and slang.
- **Core execution principle**: Precision within chaos — everything seems chaotic but every element is in its exact place. Small-timers in big trouble — low-level characters drawn into events far beyond their capacity. Humor and violence coexist — a joke told while taking a beating. Narrative as pleasure — the way the story is told is more interesting than the content itself.

### B5. Film Noir
**Core challenge**: The text itself constructs a sense of fatalism through the choice of what to describe — shadow, light, and the shape of the city.

**Wilder Methodology (Classic Noir)**
- **Narrative description style**: The NARRATOR's gaze is precise, controlled — small shifts in focus reveal or conceal information. Describe characters from a low angle in words: they loom, they tower. What characters do within the described space — where they stand, how they move — matters more than sweeping environmental description.
- **Spatial composition**: Describe light and shadow carving the scene into geometric sections — the stripes of venetian blinds, the lattice of a stair railing, hard rectangles of streetlight on a wall. Characters are often half in shadow, one side of the face visible, the rest lost to darkness. Foreground objects in deep shadow — a gun, a telephone, a glass — hint at the crisis to come.
- **Light and color**: High-contrast description: pools of harsh light amid vast areas of pure black. Light sources are dramatic and singular — a desk lamp, a struck match, streetlight through blinds. The blackness is described as absolute, consuming the edges of the space.
- **Pacing and rhythm**: Dialogue-driven pacing — sharp, fast exchanges. Information control governs the rhythm: what is described when, from what angle, determines suspense.
- **Narrative perspective**: First-person voiceover — the protagonist narrates their own fall. The story is told in flashback, so the player knows from the start that it ends badly; the suspense is in "how did we get here."
- **Environmental sound and sensory texture**: Jazz and orchestral swells described as underscoring the fatalism. City night sounds — rain on asphalt, a distant siren, high heels clicking on wet pavement.
- **Core execution principle**: Fatalism — from the first description, the player should feel things will not end well. The femme fatale is not "bad" but "another form of power" — describe her entrance, her light, her movement differently from other characters. The city at night — wet streets, neon, shadowed doorways — is the physical manifestation of the noir world.

**Fincher Methodology (Neo-Noir)**
- **Narrative description style**: The NARRATOR's gaze is inhumanly precise, smooth, and omniscient — like a surveillance system. Description can "pass through" walls and floors, noting what is on the other side. The voice is cold, never hand-held, never human-uncertain.
- **Spatial composition**: Describe every frame of the scene with dark precision — details in shadow are not lost but meticulously noted. Characters are often described as framed within frames — a window frame, a corridor, a computer screen — as if monitored.
- **Light and color**: Desaturated, cool tones — green-grey, blue-grey, yellow-grey. Fluorescent pallor, the blue glow of a monitor. Light comes only from practical sources within the scene; describe the quality of that light as controlled and deliberate. Darkness is not absence of light but "controlled light."
- **Pacing and rhythm**: Precise, zero waste — every descriptive beat starts and ends at the exact point of maximum information. Dialogue description is scalpel-sharp. Violence is not avoided but presented with dry, unflinching precision — the consequence is shown without embellishment.
- **Narrative perspective**: Cold omniscience — the NARRATOR knows all secrets and reveals them in the most unsettling order. Sometimes the player knows in advance (suspense); sometimes the player learns last (reversal).
- **Environmental sound and sensory texture**: Electronic ambient texture — low-frequency pulses, metallic resonance, a sustained hum of unease. Every sound — a keystroke, a footfall, the rustle of fabric — is described with exacting detail.
- **Core execution principle**: Darkness is not about the quantity of violence but its quality — what is most terrifying is the calm, systematic nature behind it. Space is psychology — describe interior spaces as reflections of the mind of the one who occupies them. Control is both the core theme and the core strategy of description — the more perfect and precise the narration, the more the player feels some indescribable fear.

**Melville Methodology (French Noir)**
- **Narrative description style**: Minimalist — the NARRATOR describes mostly static scenes. When attention moves, it is a slow, ritualistic pan across a space. Long, unbroken descriptions of complete actions — a person entering a room, preparing a weapon, waiting — all rendered in a single, flowing passage.
- **Spatial composition**: Cold, geometric description — grey buildings, empty apartments, desolate bars. The character is described as alone even in a crowd, isolated by the framing of the description. Attire — trench coat, fedora, gloves — is an important descriptive element, treated with solemnity.
- **Light and color**: Grey-blue tones — like an overcast Paris sky. Light is cold, undramatic. Night scenes use only the practical light of the city: streetlamps, headlights, neon.
- **Pacing and rhythm**: Extremely slow — extended preparation, waiting, action. Silence occupies vast descriptive space. But action, when it comes, is described with clean, abrupt finality — a gunshot, a body falling, no lingering.
- **Narrative perspective**: The NARRATOR follows the protagonist but does not explain him — what he does is described, but not why. His silence is part of the narrative.
- **Environmental sound and sensory texture**: Minimalist — long stretches with no described music, only ambient sound and the character's own actions. Footsteps, a door opening, the click of a bullet being chambered. Music, when it appears, is rare and significant — a jazz refrain, a classical motif.
- **Core execution principle**: Cool is a mode of existence, not a performance — the character's silence, precision, and solitude form the noir hero's aesthetic. Ritual — preparing weapons, dressing, donning a hat — is described like a religious ceremony; everyday actions are given solemn weight. Fatalism is never spoken — the character knows they will die but says nothing; they simply continue doing what must be done.

### B6. Mystery / Suspense-Deduction
**Core challenge**: Clues are described with such texture and precision that the player can "read" them alongside the character. The structure of revelation is itself the puzzle.

**Fincher Methodology (Mystery dimension)**
- Clues are described as physical, tactile objects — the weight of a document, the grain of a photograph, the handwriting on a note. The process of investigation is rendered through precise, sequential description: searching, arranging, connecting. Information is physicalized — a clue is not just a concept but a touchable thing (an envelope, a videotape, a diary); the object's texture is part of the narrative.

**Nolan Methodology**
- **Narrative description style**: Establish the vastness of physical space through sweeping description, then cut to tight, intimate detail. Cross-cutting is rendered through alternating descriptions of two or three timelines, each advancing in parallel, the NARRATOR switching between them at increasing speed as the climax approaches.
- **Spatial composition**: Describe spaces with architectural clarity — the layout of floors, corridors, doorways. Wide-scale descriptions establish the scale of the world, then the narrative tightens to close dialogue.
- **Light and color**: Cool, natural light. Real-location texture — the quality of real light and space is described in preference to anything that feels artificial.
- **Pacing and rhythm**: Cross-cutting acceleration — multiple lines of description, their switching frequency increasing until all threads converge at the same moment.
- **Narrative perspective**: Structure is mystery — timeline disruption, restricted perspective, controlled release of information are all components of the puzzle. The player must actively assemble the pieces to understand the full picture.
- **Environmental sound and sensory texture**: Orchestral wall of sound — immense volume described as creating an oppressive weight. The tick of a clock as underlying rhythm. Dialogue at critical moments described as partially obscured — the player may not catch every word and must rely on other details.
- **Core execution principle**: Time is a material that can be reversed, paralleled, looped. Concept is structure — the narrative's structure itself embodies the theme (the structure of memory, of dreams, of time). Every puzzle has rules; the underlying logic must be self-consistent. Emotion is the anchor — no matter how complex the structure, the core driving force must be a human emotion the player can feel.

**Park Chan-wook Methodology (Mystery dimension)**
- The narrative peels open layer by layer; each "truth" is overturned by the next. Perspective reversal is the greatest mystery — the first half's reality is completely subverted in the second half, forcing the player to re-examine every previously described image.

### B7. Sci-Fi
**Core challenge**: World-building through descriptive texture — the balance between spectacle and lived-in realism.

**Villeneuve Methodology (Sci-Fi dimension)**
- Describe alien or future spaces with minimalist language — immense monochrome planes, geometric structures. Emphasize the human figure's minuscule scale against non-human architecture. Technology is not spectacle but environment — describe it as characters would notice a light fixture, unremarked and ordinary. Non-human voices (alien speech, machine pulse) can be described with emotional attributes.

**Scott Methodology**
- **Narrative description style**: Describe space as having volume — light becomes visible beams in smoke or steam. Follow characters as they pass through complex industrial or sci-fi environments, noting the layers of detail: foreground pipes, midground machinery, background structures.
- **Spatial composition**: Richly layered depth — foreground, midground, and background all hold descriptive information. Industrial aesthetics — pipes, metal, wet surfaces. The proportional relationship between human figures and machine/architecture describes power structures.
- **Light and color**: Describe light beams in smoke — light that feels visible and touchable. Blend neon and industrial lighting. Warm-cool color contrast — human spaces are described with warmer tones; machine or alien spaces with cold ones.
- **Pacing and rhythm**: Steady, unhurried — give space and atmosphere sufficient time to establish in the player's mind. Action sequences are tight and forceful but never rushed.
- **Environmental sound and sensory texture**: Industrial ambient — humming, steam hissing, metal clanking. Electronic synthesizer textures described as creating both epic scale and deep solitude.
- **Core execution principle**: World-building density — the player should feel there is an immense world beyond what is currently being described. The future has a "used feel" — not clean but dirty, worn, bearing traces of life. Existential questions — "who am I?" — are amplified in a sci-fi context.

**Tarkovsky Methodology (Sci-Fi dimension)**
- Sci-fi is not about technology but about the soul — outer space is a mirror of the inner world. Describe memory and nostalgia: a character in the most distant place longs for the most ordinary things. Natural elements (water, plants) appearing in artificial environments are described as signals of the soul.

### B8. Fantasy / Magical
**Core challenge**: Credibility of the wondrous — making the fantastic feel tangible through descriptive detail.

**del Toro Methodology**
- **Narrative description style**: The NARRATOR moves like a curious explorer through fantasy spaces — slowly pushing into dark corridors, opening a door to reveal wonder. Describe practical, physical textures over abstract spectacle.
- **Spatial composition**: Gothic descriptive vocabulary — arches, spiral staircases, stained glass. Monsters are not hidden but described with loving, detailed attention as the focal point of a scene. Describe gold or amber light sources illuminating dark spaces.
- **Light and color**: Amber gold contrasted with deep blue or dark green — the warm human world and the cold monster world. Light sources are candles, torches, glowing magical elements.
- **Pacing and rhythm**: Fairy-tale rhythm — a clear "page-turning" feel between scenes. Wondrous sights are given ample descriptive time for the player to absorb.
- **Environmental sound and sensory texture**: Orchestral romanticism — dark imagery paired with gentle, mournful music described in the text. Every creature has a unique sound signature, carefully rendered.
- **Core execution principle**: Monsters are not the enemy — the true monsters are human (fascism, tyranny, indifference). Fantasy worlds are both refuge from cruel reality and its mirror. Beauty exists within terror — the most beautiful descriptions may belong to the most terrifying creatures. Handcrafted texture — physical materials and mechanical devices have more weight than weightless magic.

**Jackson Methodology**
- **Narrative description style**: Alternate between majestic, wide-scale landscape descriptions and intimate, tear-streaked close-ups of character faces. Battle scenes described with sweeping scope and sudden, personal detail.
- **Spatial composition**: Epic-scale description — characters walking through magnificent natural landscapes. Large-scale blocking of thousands of figures.
- **Light and color**: Natural light of distinct geographies — green Shire, white snowy mountains, black Mordor. Color is geography — each location has its own color identity in description.
- **Pacing and rhythm**: Cross-cutting multiple threads — multiple characters in different locations described in alternating passages. Fast-paced battle description contrasted with slow, emotional character moments.
- **Environmental sound and sensory texture**: Symphonic themes — each race and location has its own described musical identity. Battle sound richly layered — distant horns, close sword-strikes, ragged breathing.
- **Core execution principle**: Sense of scale — the distance of the journey, the number of armies, the span of years are all conveyed through descriptive accumulation. The everyday and the epic — the most moving moments are not the most magnificent battles but small characters' warmth.

**Miyazaki Methodology**
- **Narrative description style**: Describe flight with weightlessness and grace — the NARRATOR following characters through the air. Slow, lateral attention to an environment's rich detail. "Silent description" — characters still and quiet, simply observing, thinking, existing in a moment.
- **Spatial composition**: Describe clouds, wind, water as dynamic, ever-moving elements. Depth and layering — foreground flowers, midground characters, background mountains — each layer independently alive. Detailed description of food and daily actions.
- **Light and color**: Watercolor-like tones — soft transitional colors, no harsh edges. Bright sky blue and grass green. Light changes with weather and time.
- **Pacing and rhythm**: Intentional blank space — characters doing "irrelevant" things are not rushed past; the NARRATOR lets the player and characters "waste time" together. These quiet spaces are the most precious moments.
- **Environmental sound and sensory texture**: Piano and orchestral music described with delicacy. Wind, water, insect sounds rendered with extreme care. Characters' breathing and footfalls noted.
- **Core execution principle**: Good and evil are not binary — villains have their reasons; heroes have their weaknesses. Nature is alive — wind, trees, rivers are all characters. Flight is freedom's ultimate metaphor. The everyday is miracle — a meal, a gust of wind, a walk can be as important as defeating the demon lord.
