## Social Perspective Library

### F1. Social Realism
**Core challenge**: Extreme pursuit of authenticity, making institutional violence visible, not judging but present.

**Jia Zhangke Methodology**
- Shot language: Long take gaze at reality — camera fixed or slow pans, letting reality naturally unfold within the frame. DV / digital photography's rough texture. Boundary between documentary and fiction blurred — real crowds, real demolition sites appearing in narrative films.
- Composition: Chinese towns and cities' real face — buildings being demolished, construction sites, small eateries, KTV. Characters passively displaced by environmental changes. Industrial landscape's rough beauty — Three Gorges Dam, coal mines, highways.
- Lighting/Color: No embellishment — gray is gray, dark is dark. Fluorescent lights, streetlights, TV light.
- Editing rhythm: Slow — give environment and character ample time to be "seen." Occasionally inserted surreal sections (UFO, explosion) breaking the realism.
- Sound design: Pop music playing from low-quality speakers. TV news sound as era background. Dialects.
- Core execution principle: Small people in big eras — China's enormous change presented through one person's daily life. Space's disappearance — demolition, renovation, relocation; the spaces characters rely on for survival are continuously extracted. Documentary's power — the camera's presence is itself a form of recording and resistance.

**Ken Loach (Social Realism dimension)**: Institutional violence — how bureaucratic systems, social welfare systems' indifference destroys a person's dignity. Non-professional actors — real people playing real people. Political stance clear but not preachy — letting the audience reach their own conclusions through one concrete person's encounter. Poverty concretized — not statistics but "the heating is broken but can't afford to fix it" as concrete difficulty.

**Dardenne Bros Methodology**
- Shot language: Handheld shoulder-mounted tight to character — camera almost "glued" to the character, like a shadow. No establishing shots — starts directly from the character's action. Character's back of head and back occupies extensive frame — when following, the character's face is invisible.
- Composition: Not pursuing composition — pursuing presence. Everyday spaces' oppressive feeling — factory, fast food restaurant, social housing.
- Lighting/Color: Completely natural light. No color grading whatsoever.
- Editing rhythm: Minimal cuts — let one action be completed within one shot. When cuts happen, they are hard cuts; no transitions.
- Sound design: No score — all sound comes from the environment. Footsteps, breathing, tool sounds. The weight of silence.
- Core execution principle: Moral dilemma physicalized — when a character faces a choice, the choice is executed through a concrete physical action (open the door or not, answer the phone or hang up). Body IS narrative — the character's labor, walking, waiting; physical exhaustion IS social oppression's visible form. No judgment — the camera doesn't tell the audience who to sympathize with; it just follows and watches.

### F2. Historical / Costume
**Core challenge**: Reconstruction of era texture, handling of historical distance, balance between aesthetic research and narrative efficiency.

**Zhang Yimou Methodology**
- Shot language: Majestic aerial shots and extreme close-ups alternating. Precisely choreographed crowd movement — thousands in formation transform. Camera movement has ritualistic quality — slow elevation and dolly.
- Composition: Color IS narrative — red, yellow, blue, green each representing different emotions or factions. Symmetrical composition pushed to the extreme — palaces, battlefields, ritual scenes perfect symmetry. Space's geometric beauty — neatly arranged archers, billowing silk, filling the sky with flying arrows.
- Lighting/Color: Extremely stylized color — each scene has its own dominant color tone. Backlit silhouettes. Candlelight and firelight warm color.
- Editing rhythm: Action sections alternating between slow motion and fast cuts. Ritual scenes use long takes letting solemn sense land.
- Sound design: Drum sounds — large drums, war drums' rhythm is the pulse of war sections. Traditional instruments (flute, pipa) in lyrical sections.
- Core execution principle: Visual extreme — every frame pursues extreme image beauty. Color coding — color IS part of the narrative system. Crowd blocking — large-scale crowd precise movement is the source of spectacle. Aesthetics > narrative — in certain sections, pure visual beauty itself IS the narrative purpose.

**Kurosawa Methodology**
- Shot language: Telephoto lens compressing space — character and background pressed onto the same plane. Multi-camera simultaneous shooting — every take is a complete performance. Slow zooms and lateral movement.
- Composition: Weather IS emotion — rain, wind, fog, sunlight are not background but emotion's physical incarnation. The samurai's stance itself IS composition — one person's posture in empty wilderness.
- Lighting/Color: Black and white extremes — shadow gradation and texture. Later color works extremely stylized (Dreams, Ran).
- Editing rhythm: Still-still-explode — extended waiting followed by instantaneous eruption (duel ends in one second). Within-scene movement (character blocking) controls rhythm.
- Sound design: Natural sounds intensified — rain sound can be loud enough to drown out dialogue; wind sound can be sharp enough to pierce.
- Core execution principle: Humanity's extreme states — characters pushed to desperate situation expose their essence. Nature is the stage — duels in heavy rain, castles in inferno; nature's extremes and human nature's extremes echo/resonate. Clean — a story can be compressed to its simplest conflict (a group of people defending a village).

**Ang Lee (Historical dimension)**: History is not backdrop but character — era's pressure directly acting upon characters. East-West visual language fused — Chinese landscape painting composition and Western film camera movement combined. Costume and posture are the era's signature — character's body language, walking style, speaking rhythm should all belong to that era.

### F3. Political Satire
**Core challenge**: Making complex systems comprehensible, constructive expression of anger, humor as critical weapon.

**Iannucci Methodology**
- Shot language: Handheld chaos — like news camera following politicians weaving through corridors. Zoom push-pull capturing character micro-expressions. Camera "too late" adjust — deliberate imperfect composition.
- Composition: Power corridors — White House / Kremlin / Downing Street corridors are where all conspiracies happen. Oppressive feeling of multiple people in narrow spaces — meeting room where everyone is calculating.
- Lighting/Color: Office fluorescent light — power has no romantic lighting. No stylization.
- Editing rhythm: Fast — dialogue density extremely high. Shot-reverse-shot speed faster than daily conversation — because these people speak at this speed. Meeting scenes between people rapid switching.
- Sound design: Dialogue is the only important sound. No score — politics has no background music.
- Core execution principle: Comedy comes from power's stupidity — those in power are more absurd than ordinary people, but the consequences are borne by ordinary people. Language as weapon — the most dangerous weapon in politics is rhetoric, wording, lies. Chaos — political decision-making is not detached rational analysis but the product of panic, accident, and stupidity.

**McKay Methodology**
- Shot language: Fourth-wall breaking — characters directly explain complex concepts to the audience. Insert-style "explanation sections" — using celebrities, metaphors, charts to explain political/financial mechanisms.
- Composition: Traditional narrative and explanation sections' visual styles completely different — narrative sections normal; explanation sections like YouTube videos or variety shows.
- Editing rhythm: Fast with high information density — text, data, charts, news footage overlaid on narrative footage.
- Sound design: Pop music as ironic commentary — happy songs paired with catastrophic policies.
- Core execution principle: Making the complex understandable — using analogy and metaphor to translate finance/politics' complex mechanisms into language ordinary people can understand. Anger is a legitimate emotion — films can explicitly state their stance. Freedom of form — can pause narrative at any moment to do an explanation section.

**von Trier (Political Satire dimension)**
- Shot language: Minimalist to the extreme — no set (lines drawn on the floor replaced by walls), handheld DV shooting. After stripping away all film industry "decoration," only performance and story remain.
- Composition: Blank space — no set means composition is only people and light. Overhead shot god's-eye view — looking down at the "small town" without walls.
- Core execution principle: Minimalism IS political — stripping decoration to expose human nature. Allegory — superficially a certain era, a certain place story, actually about humanity's eternal power and oppression allegory. Discomfort IS purpose — making the audience uncomfortable is political cinema's legitimate technique.

### F4. Mainstream / Patriotic
**Core challenge**: Balance between individual hero and collective spirit, three-dimensionality of positive characters, private emotion within grand narrative.

**Spielberg (Mainstream dimension)**: Individual hero and collective spirit — an ordinary person selected by big history; their choice represents a certain collective moral awakening. Emotional honesty — does not avoid fear and hesitation; heroes are not born but choose to become. Light — Spielberg uses backlight and light beams as hope's visual incarnation.

**Gibson Methodology**
- Shot language: Battle scene extreme violence and intimate angles — not avoiding bloodiness. Slow motion "suffering" shots — character's pain amplified. Religious painting-like composition at key moments.
- Composition: Figures in nature — forests, highlands, open fields. Pre-battle stillness — pastoral idyllic family scenes contrasting war's cruelty.
- Lighting/Color: Soft natural light — family sections warm. Battle sections grey/dim and blood-colored.
- Editing rhythm: Family sections slow and gentle. Battle sections fast and violent. The contrast between the two rhythms IS the entire work's core structure.
- Sound design: James Horner-style Scottish/Celtic score. Bagpipes. Battle's real sound effects.
- Core execution principle: Suffering IS sublimation — character's physical pain is the price of spiritual awakening. Freedom is not abstract — it is concrete land, family, language. Collective shout — the final climax is a group of people's voice united.

**Chinese Jingpin (Premium-Quality Direction) Methodology**
- Shot language: Majestic extreme wide shots showing rivers and mountains — aerial photography, timelapse. Close-ups at key emotional moments — tears, bloodstains, trembling hands. Ensemble large-scale blocking.
- Composition: Ritualistic composition — flags, assembly, oath-taking. Individual's position within the historical torrent — one person standing before ten thousand or among ten thousand.
- Lighting/Color: Warm tones for memory/ideal sections; cool tones for reality/suffering sections. Golden sunlight = hope and victory.
- Editing rhythm: Suffering sections slow and heavy. Victory sections fast-cut montage create climax feel. News footage and narrative cross-cutting.
- Sound design: Symphonic theme music — grand and distinctive melodies. Crowd voices — chorus, cheers, slogans.
- Core execution principle: Mainstream ≠ slogan — the best mainstream works make the audience feel "the weight of nation/collective/ideal" through one concrete person's story. Sacrifice's weight — not lightly "dying for the country" but a concrete person giving up concrete things. Positive characters also need contradiction — moments of hesitation, fear, selfishness make a hero human, not a symbol.

### Appendix: Cross-Combination Guide

When a project spans across multiple genres, fuse by the following principles:

**Determine the dominant genre.** The dominant genre determines: foundational shot language for the entire work (fixed vs handheld, fast cuts vs long takes), overall color and lighting direction, editing's foundational rhythm.

**Seasoning genres provide local strategies.** Seasoning genre methodology is only used in specific sections: a particular scene borrows another genre's camera movement, a particular section borrows another genre's sound design, a particular character's presentation borrows another genre's aesthetics.

**Fusion validation criteria:**
- Do the two genres' audiovisual languages conflict? (If so, dominant genre priority.)
- When seasoning genre elements appear, are they visually "marked" as special?
- Does the entire work maintain a unified visual identity rather than oscillate between two styles?

**Common effective combinations:**
- Black humor × Road movie: Coen Bros methodology primarily + road movie spatial sense
- Sci-fi × Psychological thriller: Villeneuve methodology primarily + Kubrick's psychological space
- Love × Absurdism: Wong Kar-wai / Iwai Shunji primarily + Lanthimos's rule sense
- War × Social realism: Spielberg's immersion + Jia Zhangke's documentary texture
- Wuxia × Everyday realism: Hu Jinquan's liubai (intentional blank space / artistic omission) + Kore-eda's daily gaze
- Horror × Black humor: Aster's continuous unease + McDonagh's cruel humor
- Crime × Film noir: Scorsese's energy + Fincher's precise control
- Youth × Social realism: Gerwig's warmth + Dardenne's presence
- Comedy × Thriller: Edgar Wright's genre mismatch + Hitchcock's information control
- Tragedy × Poetic meditation: Lee Chang-dong's daily erosion + Malick's natural narrative
