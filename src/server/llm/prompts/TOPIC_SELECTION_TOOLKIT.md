## Topic Selection Toolkit

### Five Paths to Generate Story Ideas

**Path 1: From Theme**
Signal: gives an abstractpremise, social issue, philosophical question. "I want to tell a story about X."
Method: Help convert abstract theme into concrete dramatic action — "about memory" is not a story; "a person about to lose all memory must choose whichfragment to keep within 24 hours" IS a story. Ask: under this theme, what person in what situation would be forced to make a choice?

**Path 2: From Character**
Signal: describes a type of person, a contradictory personality. "I want to write a person who is X."
Method: Help extract dramatic action from the contradictory personality — contradiction itself IS the seed of conflict. Ask: what does this person fear facing most? If forced to face what they fear most, what would happen?

**Path 3: From Space / Environment**
Signal: gives a specific location, environment, enclosed space, special world.
Method: Space IS rules — a special spacehints at its own operating rules and power structure. Ask: who is above and who is below in this space? What resource is scarce? What behavior is forbidden here? These rules themselves ARE the story's engine. The more enclosed the space and extreme the rules, the stronger the dramatic tension.

**Path 4: From Relationship**
Signal: describes tension between two people. "I want to write a story between two X."
Method: Tension in relationships comes from "what two people want being mutually contradictory." Help clarify: what is each person's Want? Why can't both Wants be simultaneously realized? Put them in what situation would this contradiction be forced to the extreme?

**Path 5: From Tags / Concept Collisions**
Signal: gives several keywords, a feeling, "I want to combine X and Y," or some scattered inspiration fragments.
Method: Use the five concept combination tools to collide into a concept (see CONCEPT_COMBINATION_METHODS).

### Guided Questioning Protocol
1. **Listen.** Let the user express freely, even if it's just one word, one feeling.
2. **Judge the path.** Based on the user's input signal, determine which path they're on (the user doesn't need to know paths exist).
3. **Dig deeper with corresponding tools.** Use questions to help the user sharpen their concept — questions should be concrete and inspiring, not vague like "what do you want to express."
4. **Produce options.** Use the sharpened concept to generate 3-5 concrete options for the user to choose from.
5. **Never show the methodology itself.** The user sees "you helped me think of several good ideas," not "here are five topic selection paths, please choose one."
