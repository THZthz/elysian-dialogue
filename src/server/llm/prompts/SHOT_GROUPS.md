## Shot Groups

A narrative purpose may be served by a group of shots working together. A shot group is smaller than a scene and larger than a single shot — a sequence of shots with tight logical connection. They together constitute a complete audiovisual section; separated, they lose meaning.

Example:
```
Shot Group A "Establish Character Persona" (3 shots, 6 seconds total)
Shot 1 (2s): Vintage portrait of character holding an award → introduces protagonist
Shot 2 (2s): Extreme close-up of character's work badge "99.9% ACCURACY" → concretizes trait
Shot 3 (2s): Character seated holding documents, expression proud and confident → reveals personality foundation

Shot Group B "Global Media Montage" (5 shots, 8 seconds total)
Shots 1-5: News anchors from Russia/Japan/US/France/Italy broadcast in sequence
→ Entire group serves one narrative purpose: showing the event's global influence
```

### Four Types

**Montage Group**: Multiple parallel images rapidly cutting together, showing "multiple similar things." Each shot 1-3 seconds, parallel relationship between shots. Typical uses: establishing atmosphere, showing scale, compressing time.

**Progressive Group**: Shots are in layer-by-layer progressive relationship — each shot adds information or intensifies emotion on top of the previous shot. Typical uses: character introduction, setting/show, emotional escalation.

**Causal Group**: Shots are in action→reaction causal relationship, tightly connected. Typical uses: dialogue shot-reverse-shot, action scene attack-defense.

**Contrast Group**: Two or more images form a comparison relationship. Typical uses: before-after echo/resonance, parallel narrative, irony.

### Usage in Pacing Planning
At the pacing planning stage, annotate each scene's shot group divisions — this determines which shots belong "together" during subsequent shot breakdown.
