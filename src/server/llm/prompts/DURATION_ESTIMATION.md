## Duration Estimation & Rhythm Control

Every dramatic action, every scene should have a duration estimate. Duration is not just "how long is this scene" — it's also "what proportion of the overall rhythm does this scene occupy."

### Why Duration Estimation Is Necessary
- A scene that reads as important in the outline but only gets 30 seconds will have its impact compressed to the point that the audience barely notices
- A scene that occupies 15% of total runtime but only transmits one secondary message is a drag on rhythm
- Only by marking durations can you see rhythm problems on paper — where it's too rushed, where it drags, whether the climax has sufficient setup time

### Estimation Methods

**Dialogue estimation**: ~200-250 characters of dialogue + action description ≈ 1 minute of screen time.

**Scene estimation rules**:
- Pure dialogue scenes: estimate by dialogue word count
- Action / montage scenes: estimate by number of images (each independent image ≈ 3-5 seconds)
- Silence / emotional scenes: easy to underestimate — a character standing there doing nothing may need 10-15 seconds in the final cut to carry sufficient emotional weight
- Transitions: don't forget to account for them, typically 2-5 seconds each

### Scene Duration Annotation Format
```
【Scene 3: Hospital Corridor / Night / Fluorescent lights humming】(approx. 1 min 30 sec)
  Plot rhythm: Medium (a key conversation)
  Emotional rhythm: Heavy (character faces the truth for the first time)
```

### Full Runtime Budget
At the outline stage, make a rough runtime budget to ensure proportional balance across sections.

### Rhythm Diagnostics
At the script doctor stage, use the runtime budget to check:
- Is the climax section long enough? (Too short makes the climax feel compressed)
- Is the setup too long? (Audience waits too long before entering the main story)
- Are there any sections of 3+ consecutive minutes with a single emotional tone? (Whether tension or relaxation, sustained too long becomes ineffective)
- Is there sufficient breathing room between two high-emotion scenes?
- Does the longest single scene have sufficient reason to occupy that much duration?
