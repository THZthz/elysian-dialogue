## Inter-Episode Rhythm Management

N episodes cannot all be the same emotional tone. Rhythm needs waves.

### 10 Mood Types per Episode
Choose a dominant emotion for each episode (can mix, but should have a clear dominant):

- **Setup type**: Audience adapts to the world, meets characters (typically early episodes)
- **Light type**: Strong comedy feel, character interaction primarily, audience accumulates positive feeling/goodwill
- **Tension type**: Core conflict escalates, pressure increases
- **Breathing type**: Rhythm slows, character relationships deepen, provides emotional anchor points
- **Pivot type**: Major event occurs, the overall landscape/structure changes
- **Pressure type**: Internal and external pressure continues tightening, almost no respite
- **Collapse type**: All is lost, lowest point
- **Dark night type**: Character confronts Ghost and Lie directly
- **Counterattack type**: Character counterattacks with new understanding
- **Climax type**: Resolution of the season's core conflict

### Wave Principles
- No 3+ consecutive episodes of the same tone
- Tension/pressure episodes need at least 1 breathing or light episode afterward
- Collapse and dark night can be consecutive (forming "the valley"), but the valley before and after must have contrast
- Mid-season (40-60% position) is suitable for a pivot — changing the direction of the second half
- Breathing episodes are not wasted episodes — their function is to deepen character relationships and advance B Plot

### Reference Rhythm Template (by percentage)
- First 10%: Setup
- 10-20%: Light / Setup
- 20-30%: First tension
- 30-40%: Breathing
- 40-50%: Pivot (mid-season major event)
- 50-60%: Transition + escalation
- 60-70%: Pressure
- 70-75%: Breathing or false climax
- 75-85%: Collapse / Dark night
- 85-95%: Counterattack
- Final 5-10%: Climax + resolution
