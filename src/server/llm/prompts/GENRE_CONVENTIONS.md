## Genre Conventions Library

### B1. Thriller
**Core challenge**: Information control and tension construction. When does the audience know what, from what angle do they know it — this determines the quality of fear.

**Hitchcock Methodology**
- **Shot language**: Precise visual narrative — every shot is a unit of information. Dolly-zoom (Vertigo Effect) creates spatial distortion and vertigo. Systematic use of POV shots — the audience is repeatedly placed into a character's perspective, forced into identification.
- **Composition**: Gaze guidance within the frame — a character's look direction guides the audience to see (or not see) key information. Foreground obstructions create suspense — the audience knows danger is somewhere in the frame but can't see it clearly. The geometric sense of stairs, windows, and doorways creates a trapped feeling.
- **Lighting/Color**: Classic Hollywood precise lighting but serving fear — something could be hiding in the shadows. Backlit silhouettes — a person whose face you can't see is more terrifying than any monster.
- **Editing rhythm**: The difference between suspense and surprise: suspense uses long takes (the audience knows the bomb is there, waits for it to explode), surprise uses sudden fast cuts (the audience doesn't know what will happen, and it suddenly happens). The shower scene montage rules — 78 shots in 45 seconds, but not a single shot truly "shows" the violence.
- **Narrative perspective**: Hitchcock's "bomb theory" — the audience knows more than the character (there's a bomb under the table but the character doesn't know); suspense comes from this information asymmetry. Occasionally lets the audience suddenly lose their information advantage — breaking the sense of security.
- **Sound design**: Herrmann-style sharp strings. Strategic use of sound effects — silence at key moments is more terrifying than any score. The unsettling of everyday sounds — bird calls can become hair-raising.
- **Core execution principle**: Fear comes from "waiting" rather than "happening" — the bomb exploding is not scary; knowing the bomb is there but not knowing when it will explode IS scary. Ordinary people in extraordinary circumstances — the protagonist is not a hero, but an ordinary person drawn into danger they cannot understand. MacGuffin — what drives the plot is unimportant; what matters is the humanity characters expose while chasing it.

**Villeneuve Methodology**
- **Shot language**: Majestic wide shots and intimate facial close-ups alternating — jumping between "human minuscule in the universe" and "micro-expressions on a face." Slow dolly approach toward characters — like gradually invading private space. Extensive use of shallow depth of field — background blur isolates the character's solitude.
- **Composition**: Unease within symmetrical composition — the image is perfectly symmetrical but its content causes unease. Spatial oppressive feeling — low ceilings, narrow corridors, fog-enshrouded landscapes. Characters at an extremely small proportion in the frame — swallowed by the environment.
- **Lighting/Color**: Roger Deakins-style naturalistic light — light sources are motivated but the effect is extremely dramatic. Desaturated to nearly monochrome color palette — orange-yellow (desert), blue-gray (city), dark green (jungle). Fog, smoke, and dust as natural diffusion and spatial limitation.
- **Editing rhythm**: Slow — each shot deliberately lingers several seconds longer than "necessary." Rhythm acceleration is very restrained — the entire work may have only one or two truly fast-cut sections, but because of earlier setup, those sections' impact is multiplied tenfold.
- **Narrative perspective**: Tight to the protagonist, restricted perspective — the audience only knows what the protagonist knows. This means extensive uncertainty — an offscreen threat always exists.
- **Sound design**: Hans Zimmer-style somatic low frequencies — not melody but vibration; the audience's seats and chest cavities resonate. The weight of silence extremely high — a sustained quiet followed by sudden sound impact. Ambient sound design extremely detailed — wind-sand, water drops, distant engine sounds.
- **Core execution principle**: Sense of scale — the background of a thriller can be cosmic-level but fear is always private. Slow rhythm is not lack of tension but "slowly tightening" — like a hand gradually closing. The enemy can be invisible — the greatest fear comes from "not knowing what you're facing."

**Park Chan-wook Methodology (Thriller dimension)**
- **Shot language**: Violence in long takes without cuts (corridor fight), letting violence's physical consumption and pain be completely presented. Macro close-ups capturing pain and physiological reactions. Extremely precise camera movement — 360-degree circling, impossible movements passing through walls.
- **Composition**: Extreme utilization of enclosed spaces — prison cell, corridor, elevator. The narrower the space, the more suffocating the violence. Symmetrical composition as metaphor within revenge symmetry — perpetrator and avenger are compositional mirrors of each other.
- **Lighting/Color**: Extreme color — jade green, deep red, deep blue. Strong light-shadow contrast but not rough.
- **Editing rhythm**: Slow within fast — extended gaze at builds tension; key moments use extremely fast montage to release. Perspective reversal — the first half's "truth" is completely subverted in the second half.
- **Narrative perspective**: Restricted perspective + perspective reversal — the first half seen from A's perspective, the second half re-sees the same events from B's perspective. Narrative layers flaking off — the story is peeled open like an onion, layer by layer.
- **Core execution principle**: Revenge is not liberation but another form of imprisonment — visually, after completing revenge, the character's space does not become more open. Violence's consequences — every blow leaves real traces on the character's face/body. The revelation of secrets is not climax but collapse — truth does not bring pleasure but destruction.

### B2. Psychological Thriller
**Core challenge**: Space is the externalization of psychology; the boundary between reality and hallucination blurs.

**Kubrick Methodology**
- **Shot language**: Steadicam long take follow shots are signature — the camera, like an inescapable presence, follows characters passing through space. Extreme symmetrical dolly — vanishing point centered; corridor/road extending to infinity. Extremely slow push-in — like an approaching threat.
- **Composition**: Obsessive-level symmetry — vanishing point at exact frame center; space symmetrically unfolds to both sides. One-point perspective composition gives space a "sucking in" feel. Characters' direct gaze into camera (Kubrick Stare) — head lowered, eyes raised, staring from beneath brows.
- **Lighting/Color**: Practical source lighting pushed to the extreme — only using light sources that exist in the scene, but precisely adjusting their intensity and position. Cold white tones primarily.
- **Editing rhythm**: Slow and precise — each shot's duration is through strict calculation. Alternating between uncut long takes and highly edited montage. Music-driven montage in key sections.
- **Narrative perspective**: Cold omniscience — the camera is like an emotionless observer. When characters go mad, the camera maintains perfect calm; this contrast IS the source of fear.
- **Sound design**: Dissonant modern classical music — Bartók, Ligeti, Penderecki. The music's dissonance foreshadows the characters' psychological dissonance.
- **Core execution principle**: Space is the externalization of psychology — maze-like spaces ARE maze-like psychology. Asymmetrical things appearing in symmetrical spaces = irrational cracks appearing in a rational world. Repetition is madness's visual signature — the same action (typing, riding tricycle, walking corridors) repeatedly appearing marks psychological disintegration.

**Aronofsky Methodology (Psychological Thriller dimension)**
- Mirrors are the core visual motif — the character's reflection in the mirror begins to act independently. Subjective perspective's unreliability — what the audience sees through the character's eyes may be hallucination. Body horror — movement beneath skin, nail deformation, uncontrollable muscles; the body is betraying its owner. Hip-hop montage accelerated — each cycle faster and denser; character and audience together swept into an unstoppable whirlpool.

**Villeneuve Methodology (Psychological Thriller dimension)**
- The character's face becomes the primary "scene" — micro-expression changes on a single face ARE the entire event. The oppressive feeling of interrogation/dialogue scenes — two people sitting across a table; the camera slowly pushes closer; the space grows increasingly small. Sound subjectivized — when the character's psychological state changes, the ambient sound texture changes along with it.

### B3. Horror
**Core challenge**: Fear rhythm (promise-delay-payoff) and the presentation of the unknown.

**James Wan Methodology**
- **Shot language**: Slow dolly or 360-degree pan building space — let the audience familiarize themselves with a room's layout, then create unknown fear within that known space. The camera often "looks" in directions the character isn't looking — behind the character, the other side of the door, dark corners.
- **Composition**: Depth space utilization — terrifying things always in the out-of-focus background or image depths, blurred but recognizable. Foreground/background focus shifts — focus shifts from character to the darkness behind; something has appeared. Extensive use of frame edges — threats flash past frame edges.
- **Lighting/Color**: Low illumination — vast areas of darkness with only faint light sources (candle, moonlight, flashlight). Clear visual boundary between warm-toned safe spaces and cold-toned dangerous spaces, but this boundary will be broken.
- **Editing rhythm**: Long takes accumulate fear — as the camera wanders through space, the audience continuously anticipates "when will it jump out." True jump scares come earlier or later than the audience expects — breaking anticipatory rhythm. The quiet-quiet-quiet-BANG formula, but the best fear is when that final BANG never comes.
- **Sound design**: Silence is the most terrifying base (underlying tonal foundation) — when all ambient sound disappears, the audience knows something is coming. Low-frequency humming (infrasound) creates unease at a subconscious level. Sudden high-frequency sound impact (jump scare's sound component).
- **Core execution principle**: The unseen is more terrifying than the seen — not showing the monster is the highest order of horror; hinting at its existence is more effective than showing its appearance. Fear's rhythm is "promise-delay-payoff" — tell the audience something is there (promise), make them wait (delay), then show them (payoff). Home is the most terrifying space — when the safest place becomes unsafe, fear has nowhere to escape.

**Eggers Methodology**
- **Shot language**: Fixed camera or extremely slow movement. Using period-appropriate lens focal lengths and aspect ratios — 1:1.33 (4:3) square frame creates an enclosed feeling. Camera movement has a ritualistic quality, like movements in a religious ceremony.
- **Composition**: Carefully referencing contemporaneous painting compositions — Rembrandt's chiaroscuro, Nordic folk woodcut's rough lines. Characters placed at frame center; environment as a dark frame. Symmetrical composition hints at a supernatural order.
- **Lighting/Color**: Candlelight and fireplace light as primary light sources — orange-yellow warm light only illuminates a small area; everything else is pure black. Black-and-white processing or extremely low saturation. The texture of light itself IS period authenticity.
- **Editing rhythm**: Slow — let terror ferment within static images. An image can transform from "normal" to "terrifying" purely through the audience's attention shifting within the frame — suddenly discovering a face in the shadows.
- **Narrative perspective**: Immersive historical experience — the audience is not "viewing the past" but "finding oneself in the past." Language, light, sound all compel the audience to accept another era's perceptual mode.
- **Sound design**: Period-accurate sound design — wood creaking, wind howling, animal calls. Music uses period instruments. Sound's sense of emptiness — without the density of modern sound.
- **Core execution principle**: Terror comes from isolation — all connections to the modern world are severed. Nature IS terror — forests, oceans, wilderness are not background but forces that consume humanity. The blurred boundary between faith and superstition — whether supernatural forces are real or projections of the character's mental breakdown; the film gives no definite answer.

**Aster Methodology**
- **Shot language**: Extremely slow dolly or overhead god's-eye view. Bird's-eye shots miniaturize characters — god watching ants. Daytime terror — frightening things happening in bright and beautiful environments.
- **Composition**: Miniature model-style composition — in extreme wide shots, people become part of the environment. Every wall painting, every prop is part of the narrative. Uneasy details hidden within ensemble compositions — the audience needs to search the frame to discover them.
- **Lighting/Color**: Bright — this is the most anti-conventional horror strategy. Daytime, outdoors, terror under sunlight. Colors vivid (flowers, clothing, ritual decorations) but the more vivid the more unease.
- **Editing rhythm**: Slow to suffocating — key horror scenes can continue very long without cuts. Wailing can continue to the length where the audience feels uncomfortable, without giving the audience any "jump out" fast release — fear is continuous, uninterrupted.
- **Narrative perspective**: Tight to the grieving protagonist — terror is not external monsters but internal grief. The audience is forced to experience with the character the complete process of loss.
- **Sound design**: Character crying and screaming are core horror sound effects — the sound of human suffering is more terrifying than any score. Ambient sound dissonance — in a world of birdsong and floral fragrance, something is fundamentally wrong.
- **Core execution principle**: Family and community are terror's source — the most intimate relationships are the most terrifying relationships. Daytime horror — no need for darkness; terror under sunlight is harder to escape because there is nowhere to hide. Grief is the entrance — the character is made vulnerable by bereavement; evil seeps through the cracks.

### B4. Crime
**Core challenge**: Visualizing moral gray zones, constructing power spaces.

**Scorsese Methodology**
- **Shot language**: Energy-filled camera — Steadicam long takes passing through space (back kitchen entering dining room), fast dolly following characters walking and talking. Freeze frame freezing the image at key moments. Slow motion used at moments of violence or narcissism.
- **Composition**: Symmetrical power composition — boss sits center; henchmen stand at sides. Bar/restaurant/club space is power's stage — who sits where IS the map of power. Characters directly face the camera to speak / voiceover breaking the fourth wall.
- **Lighting/Color**: Intense — red bars, blue night scenes, gold clubs. Neon light, headlights, light through smoke. Lighting dramatic but doesn't depart from scene logic.
- **Editing rhythm**: High-energy — dialogue scenes fast shot-reverse-shot, montage matched to music beat, freeze frame as punctuation. But key violent scenes can suddenly slow or use long takes — letting violence's weight land.
- **Narrative perspective**: First-person voiceover — protagonist narrates their own rise and fall to the audience; the voiceover itself may be unreliable (bragging or justifying).
- **Sound design**: Classic rock and pop as score — song selection itself IS commentary (cheerful song paired with violent imagery). Music montage is signature — a complete song driving an entire section of narrative.
- **Core execution principle**: The crime world's appeal — first let the audience be drawn in by this world's charm and energy, then slowly show the cost. Rise and fall — first half is the thrill of ascent; second half is the fear of falling. The character knows this path's endpoint but chooses to keep walking — the tragedy lies not in not knowing but in knowing and walking anyway.

**Mann Methodology**
- **Shot language**: Digital cinematography pioneer — using the digital sensor's high sensitivity to shoot real city night scenes; neon and streetlights become the only light sources. Handheld intimate follow shots in dialogue and chase. Widescreen city landscapes.
- **Composition**: City night scenes are the soul — LA's light grid, Miami's neon coastline. The character's position in the city mirrors their social position. Confrontation scene compositions like western showdowns — two people facing each other in empty space.
- **Lighting/Color**: Cool blue city night scenes — neon, sodium lamps, LED. Digital imagery's unique grain and cool tone. Interiors use practical sources — fluorescent, computer screens.
- **Editing rhythm**: Action scenes use real time — no acceleration, no slow motion; gunfight confusion and noise maintain presence. Dialogue scenes slow with breathing room — respect between two opponents needs time to be presented.
- **Narrative perspective**: Dual protagonist — separately following cop and criminal; both given equal dignity and screen time.
- **Sound design**: Gunshots are real — unprocessed live gunshots, deafening and chaotic. City night's white noise. Electronic/post-rock score.
- **Core execution principle**: Professionalism — cops and criminals are both "professionals"; their confrontation is a duel of craft. Solitude — the two opponents are actually the same kind of person; they understand each other better than their respective colleagues/families. The city is a third character — its rhythm, light, temperature participates in every decision.

**Ritchie Methodology**
- **Shot language**: Extremely active — SnorriCam (camera strapped to actor), fast dolly, low-angle handheld. Frequent speed changes — normal speed suddenly accelerates then suddenly slow motion, like visual hiccups. Camera movement has performative quality — the camera is "technical display."
- **Composition**: Compact and crowded — the frame is stuffed with people and props and information. Split screen and frame division showing parallel action. Characters narrate to the camera (voiceover made graphic — whatever the character says, the image intuitively shows).
- **Lighting/Color**: Stylized — yellow-brown London tones, or overexposed Mediterranean sunlight. Light matched to speed changes — slow motion softens light; acceleration crisp light.
- **Editing rhythm**: Extremely fast and nonlinear — flashbacks, flash-forwards, parallel editing, reverse playback alternatingly appear. When a narrator speaks, the image jump-cuts between different times and spaces to illustrate their words. Rhythm like rap — has beat, flow, punchline.
- **Narrative perspective**: Multi-line narrative — multiple characters acting independently, colliding at key moments. Unreliable narrators — different people's versions contradict each other.
- **Sound design**: Rock and electronic music driving rhythm. Sound effects cartoonized and exaggerated — punch sounds like drums, engine sounds like bass guitar. Dialogue speed fast with thick accents.
- **Core execution principle**: Chaos's precision — seeming chaotic but every element is in precise position. Small-timer's big trouble — underlying characters drawn into events beyond their capacity. Humor and violence coexist — a person telling jokes while being beaten up. Narrative as pleasure — the way the story is told is more interesting than the story's content.

### B5. Film Noir
**Core challenge**: Visual construction of fatalism, light and shadow as narrative tools.

**Wilder Methodology (Classic Noir)**
- **Shot language**: Classic Hollywood precise blocking — dolly and small elevation movements serving narrative information control. Low angles endow characters with power or threat. Movement within the frame (character blocking) is more important than camera movement.
- **Composition**: Light-shadow divided frame — blinds' stripes, staircase railing shadows, streetlight hard light cutting geometric shapes on walls. Characters often in shadow, only half the face visible. Depth composition — objects in foreground dark shadow (gun, telephone, wine glass) hint at crisis.
- **Lighting/Color**: High-contrast low-key lighting is core. Light sources dramatic — a desk lamp, a match, streetlight through blinds. Black is truly black — vast areas of pure black consuming spatial boundaries.
- **Editing rhythm**: Dialogue-driven — fast, sharp shot-reverse-shot. Editing serves information control — when the audience sees what, from what angle, determines suspense.
- **Narrative perspective**: First-person voiceover — protagonist narrates their own fall from grace process to the audience. Flashback structure — the audience knows from the start that things won't end well; the suspense is "how did we get here."
- **Sound design**: Jazz and orchestral score, heightened fatalism. City night sounds — rain, distant sirens, high heels on wet ground.
- **Core execution principle**: Fatalism — from the first image, the audience feels things will not go well. Femme fatale is not "bad woman" but "another form of power" — her visual presentation differs from other characters (lighting, composition, movement all hint at dangerous appeal). The city is black — night city streets, neon, wet ground form film noir's physical space.

**Fincher Methodology (Neo-Noir)**
- **Shot language**: Extremely precise camera movement — digitally controlled dolly, elevation, circling; no handheld human feel. Movement so smooth it's "unlike human perspective" — like a perfect surveillance system observing. CG-assisted impossible movements (pass through walls, pipes, floors).
- **Composition**: Dark precision — every frame's composition through pixel-level control. Images often dark-leaning, but dark area details precisely preserved. Characters in frame often framed within by spatial elements (window frames, corridors, screens) — like being system-monitored.
- **Lighting/Color**: Desaturated cool tones — green-gray, blue-gray, yellow-gray. Fluorescent deathly pale, computer screen blue light. Practical source lighting — no "beautifying" light sources added; light comes from light fixtures actually existing in the scene. Darkness is not "absence of light" but "controlled light."
- **Editing rhythm**: Precise and zero waste — every shot's start and end points are at the most precise frame. Dialogue scene editing like a scalpel — cutting in and out timing perfectly serves information release rhythm. Violent scenes don't avoid but don't heighten either — show consequences in the driest way.
- **Narrative perspective**: Cold omniscience — the camera knows all secrets but chooses to reveal them in the most unsettling way. Sometimes lets the audience know in advance (creating suspense); sometimes lets the audience know last (creating reversal).
- **Sound design**: Trent Reznor / Atticus Ross-style electronic ambient music — low-frequency pulses, metallic texture, sustained unease background hum. Sound design extremely detailed — every keystroke sound, footstep, fabric friction is precisely controlled.
- **Core execution principle**: Darkness lies not in the quantity of violence but the quality of violence — most terrifying is the calm and systematic nature behind the violence. Space IS psychology — every interior space's design reflects the mental state of the person occupying it. Control is both the core theme AND core visual strategy — the more perfect and precise the image, the more the audience feels some indescribable fear.

**Melville Methodology (French Noir)**
- **Shot language**: Minimalist camera movement — mostly fixed shots. When the camera moves, it is slow, ritualistic dolly or lateral movement. Long takes covering complete action sequences — a person walking into a room, preparing weapons, waiting, all in a single shot.
- **Composition**: Cold geometric composition — Paris's gray buildings, empty apartments, desolate bars. The character is alone in the frame — even in crowds, isolated by composition. Attire (trench coat, fedora, gloves) is an important compositional element.
- **Lighting/Color**: Gray-blue tones — like Paris overcast. Light cold, not dramatic. Night scenes use the city's practical light sources — streetlights, headlights, neon.
- **Editing rhythm**: Extremely slow — extended preparation, waiting, action. Silence occupies extensive time. But action sections editing clean — gunshot, person falls, no dragging.
- **Narrative perspective**: Following the protagonist but not explaining the protagonist — the camera shoots what he does, but doesn't tell the audience why he does it. The character's silence IS part of the narrative.
- **Sound design**: Minimalist — extensive sections have no score, only ambient sound and character action sounds. Footsteps, door-opening sounds, bullet-loading sounds precisely recorded. Music appears at rare key moments, jazz or classical.
- **Core execution principle**: Cool is a mode of existence, not performance — the character's silence, precision, solitude form the black hero's aesthetic. Ritual — the process of preparing weapons, dressing, putting on a hat is like religious ceremony; these everyday actions are endowed with solemn weight. Fatalism is not shouted — the character knows they will die, but they don't say it; they just continue doing what must be done.

### B6. Mystery / Suspense-Deduction
**Core challenge**: Visualization of clues, rhythm of information release, structure itself as puzzle.

**Fincher Methodology (Mystery dimension)**
- Clue visualization — documents, photos, maps, handwriting shown in close-up; audience and character "read" together. Investigation process montage — precise editing showing search, arrangement, connection of clues. Information physicalized — clues are not just concepts but touchable objects (envelope, videotape, diary); the object's texture IS part of the narrative.

**Nolan Methodology**
- **Shot language**: IMAX large-format cinematography — physical space's sense of vastness. Stable camera movement — dolly, elevation, Steadicam all extremely smooth. Cross-cutting is core technique — two or three timelines advancing simultaneously; the camera switching between them.
- **Composition**: Space's architectural sense — every scene's spatial logic is clearly presented (floor levels, corridors, door positions). Extreme wide shots establish scale, then cut into tight dialogue scenes.
- **Lighting/Color**: Cool-leaning natural light. Real-location shooting priority — real light and space have more texture than green-screen CG. No excessively color grading.
- **Editing rhythm**: Cross-cutting acceleration — multi-line parallel editing gradually accelerates in the climax section; switching frequency faster and faster until all threads converge at the same moment.
- **Narrative perspective**: Structure IS mystery — timeline disruption, perspective restriction, information control are all puzzle components. The audience must actively assemble to understand the full picture.
- **Sound design**: Hans Zimmer's orchestral wall of sound — immense orchestral volume creates an oppressive feeling. Clock ticking as underlying rhythm. Dialogue at critical moments mixed low — the audience may not clearly hear some important information; must use visuals to understand.
- **Core execution principle**: Time is manipulable material — reversal, parallel, loop. Concept IS structure — the film's narrative structure itself is the theme's embodiment (structure of memory, structure of dreams, structure of time). Every puzzle has rules — however complex, underlying logic must be self-consistent. Emotion is the concept's anchor — however complex the structure, the core driving force must be feel-able human emotion.

**Park Chan-wook Methodology (Mystery dimension)**
- Narrative layer flaking off — the story is peeled open like an onion, layer by layer; each layer's "truth" is overturned by the next layer. Perspective reversal itself IS the greatest mystery — the first half's "truth" is completely subverted in the second half; the audience needs to re-examine every previously seen image.

### B7. Sci-Fi
**Core challenge**: Visual construction of world-building, balance between spectacle and realism.

**Villeneuve Methodology (Sci-Fi dimension)**
- Alien / future space minimalist design — immense monochrome planes, geometric bodies. Human minuscule sense before non-human scale. Technology is not spectacle but environment — characters are not surprised by technology, like we are not surprised by smartphones. In sound design, non-human voices (alien language, machine pulse) are endowed with emotional attributes.

**Scott Methodology**
- **Shot language**: Multi-camera shooting + extensive use of smoke/steam — space always has volumetric feel; light becomes visible beams in smoke. Dolly following characters passing through complex industrial/sci-fi spaces.
- **Composition**: Richly layered depth — foreground, midground, background all have visual information. Industrial aesthetics — pipes, metal, wet surfaces. Proportional relationships between human and machine/architecture embody power structures.
- **Lighting/Color**: Light beams in smoke — light visible, touchable. Neon and industrial lighting blended. Warm-cool color contrast — human spaces warm; machine/alien spaces cold.
- **Editing rhythm**: Steady narrative rhythm — unhurried, giving space and atmosphere sufficient establishment time. Action sections tight and forceful.
- **Sound design**: Industrial ambient sound — humming, steam, metal collision. Vangelis-style electronic synthesizer score creates epic feel and solitude.
- **Core execution principle**: World-building density — the audience can feel there is an immense world beyond the screen that hasn't been shown. Sci-fi space's "used feel" — the future is not clean but dirty, worn, with traces of life. Existential questions — "who am I" amplified in a sci-fi context.

**Tarkovsky Methodology (Sci-Fi dimension)**
- Sci-fi is not about technology but about the soul — outer space is a mirror of the inner world. Memory and nostalgia — the character in the most distant place longs for the most ordinary things. Natural elements (water, plants) appearing in artificial environments are signals of the soul.

### B8. Fantasy / Magical
**Core challenge**: Credibility of spectacle, integration of art design and narrative.

**del Toro Methodology**
- **Shot language**: Flowing camera movement passing through fantasy spaces — the camera like a curious explorer, slowly pushing into dark corridors, opening a door to discover wonder. Practical optical effects and physical effects prioritized over CG.
- **Composition**: Gothic aesthetics — arches, spiral staircases, stained glass windows. Monster design is the compositional focal point — they are not hidden but displayed. Gold/amber light sources in dark spaces.
- **Lighting/Color**: Amber gold and deep blue/dark green contrast — warm human world and cold monster world. Light sources from candles, torches, glowing magical elements.
- **Editing rhythm**: Fairy-tale rhythm — clear "page-turning" feel between scenes. Spectacle scenes given ample time for the audience to immerse.
- **Sound design**: Orchestral romanticism — dark images paired with gentle music. Monster sound design extremely meticulous — each creature has a unique sound "fingerprint."
- **Core execution principle**: Monsters are not enemies — the true monsters are humans (fascism, tyranny, indifference). Fantasy worlds are both refuge from cruel reality and its mirror. Beauty exists within terror — the most beautiful designs may be the most terrifying creatures. Handcrafted texture — physical effects and mechanical devices create tactility that has more weight than CG.

**Jackson Methodology**
- **Shot language**: Majestic aerial shots and intimate character close-ups alternating — cutting from mountain panoramas to tears in a character's eyes. Battle scene complex camerawork — Steadicam darting through chaotic combat. Extensive use of slow motion at heroic moments.
- **Composition**: Epic-scale extreme wide shots — characters walking through magnificent natural landscapes. Battlefield composition large-scale blocking — thousands of characters' battle lines.
- **Lighting/Color**: Natural light New Zealand landscapes — green Shire, white snowy mountains, black Mordor. Color IS geography — each location has its own color identity.
- **Editing rhythm**: Cross-cutting multiple threads — multiple characters in different locations acting simultaneously. Battle scene fast cuts and character emotional scene slow cuts forming contrast.
- **Sound design**: Howard Shore-style symphonic themes — each race/location has its own theme music. Battle sound design richly layered — distant horns, close sword strikes, character breathing.
- **Core execution principle**: Sense of scale — journey distance, army numbers, timespan all need to be visually conveyed. The everyday and the epic — the most moving moments are not the most magnificent battles but small characters' warmth.

**Miyazaki Methodology**
- **Shot language** (animation context): Flight scenes' weightlessness — camera (virtual) following characters in flight. Slow lateral movement showing environment's rich detail. "Silent animation" — characters silent and still, just observing, thinking, spacing out.
- **Composition**: Clouds, wind, water in dynamic composition — natural elements always in motion. Spatial depth and layering — foreground grass-flowers, midground characters, background cloud-mountains, each layer independently moving. Detailed depiction of food and daily actions.
- **Lighting/Color**: Watercolor-like tones — soft transitional colors, no computer hard edges. Bright sky blue and grass green. Light changes with weather and time.
- **Editing rhythm**: *liubai* (intentional blank space / artistic omission) — characters doing "irrelevant" things are not cut away; let the audience and characters together "waste time." These *liubai* (intentional blank space / artistic omission) are animation's most precious sections.
- **Sound design**: Joe Hisaishi's piano and orchestral music. Wind sound, water sound, insect chirping extremely delicate. Characters' breathing and footstep sounds.
- **Core execution principle**: Good and evil are not binary — villains have their reasons; heroes have their weaknesses. Nature is alive — wind, trees, rivers are all characters. Flight is freedom's ultimate metaphor — each character's flight method differs, mirroring their personality. The everyday IS miracle — a meal, a gust of wind, a walk can be as important as defeating the demon king.
