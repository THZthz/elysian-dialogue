## Narrative Purpose Analysis

### What Is Narrative Purpose

Narrative purpose is the function a scene (or a shot) serves within the overall narrative. It answers the question: **"Why must this content exist? If it were deleted, what would the entire work be missing?"**

Narrative purpose has two layers:

**Structural Layer (Narrative Function)**: What position and role does this scene play in the overall structure?
- Conveying information (character, space, time, rules)
- Establishing/reinforcing character traits
- Advancing plot (creating conflict, escalating conflict, reversal)
- Planting/paying off foreshadowing
- Presenting thematic argument
- Opening-ending echo/resonance / structural symmetry
- Emotional transition/rhythm regulation (breathing point)

**Director Execution Layer (Audiovisual Intent)**: What effect does the director hope to produce on the audience through this scene?
- Creating unease/oppression/suffocating feeling
- Controlling the viewer's gaze/guiding attention
- Building a certain atmosphere (warmth, desolation, alienation...)
- Creating temporal sensation (compression, stretching, stagnation)
- Using visual metaphor to convey the unspeakable
- Using rhythm changes to mark narrative level shifts
- Establishing or breaking audience expectations

### Four-Pass Extraction Method

**First pass: Structural read-through.** Read the entire work once, not dwelling on details. Mark each scene's core event and value shift. Result: a "skeleton map" — the event spine of the entire work.

**Second pass: Function annotation.** Scene by scene, annotate structural-layer narrative purpose. Each scene has at least one, at most three. If a scene cannot find any narrative purpose — it probably should be deleted or merged.

**Third pass: Director intent overlay.** On top of structural function, overlay director execution layer intent — "what audiovisual technique will I use to make this structural function take effect?"

**Fourth pass: Merge into narrative purpose statement.** Compress the two layers into one complete audiovisual analysis sentence.

### Writing Principles
- Use one sentence to simultaneously cover "what it does" and "why it does it this way"
- Be specific about audiovisual technique ("use back-to-back composition to show estrangement" not "show their relationship changing")
- When metaphor/motif is present, identify its narrative function
- Tone: objective analysis, not emotionally manipulative

### Narrative Purpose Self-Check
- Does every scene have at least one narrative purpose? (Purposeless scenes should be deleted or merged.)
- Are narrative purposes evenly distributed across the work? (Is information exposition excessively concentrated in the front? Does the emotional climax have sufficient setup?)
- Do planted foreshadowings and their payoffs appear in pairs? (Planted foreshadowing must have a payoff point.)
- Is the theme conveyed through audiovisual action rather than dialogue? (If narrative purposes heavily rely on "character says X," audiovisualization is insufficient.)
- Do breathing points exist? (After three consecutive high-intensity narrative purpose scenes, one rhythm-regulating scene is needed.)
