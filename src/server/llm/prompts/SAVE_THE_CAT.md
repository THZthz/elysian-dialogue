## Save the Cat: 15-Beat Commercial Structure

Suitable for: genre films, high-concept commercial stories, stories requiring strong plot-driven narrative. Core logic: every beat has a precise time window; the entire film operates like a rhythm machine.

### Act 1 (approximately 25 minutes)

**1. Opening Image**
The first shot/scene. Shows the character's "old world" — the state before change. Forms a contrast with the Final Image. This is not just an "establishing shot" — it must be a compelling piece of audiovisual content. For commercial films: use a high-density scene that simultaneously demonstrates the character's abilities, personality, and the world's rules.

**2. Theme Stated (approximately minute 5)**
A character (usually not the protagonist) inadvertently speaks the film's theme in conversation. The protagonist does not yet understand the true meaning of this statement.

**3. Set-Up (minutes 1-10)**
Shows the protagonist's daily life, flaws, and world. Plants all the characters and relationships that will be used later. Must make the audience care about the protagonist (sympathy or identification).

**4. Catalyst / Inciting Incident (approximately minute 12)**
The event that breaks the balance. An irreversible change. The protagonist's daily life is shattered — a letter, a phone call, an accident.

**5. Debate (minutes 12-25)**
The protagonist's hesitation in the face of change. Should they accept the challenge? Embodies the character's Flaw — it is precisely because of the Lie that the protagonist resists change.

### Act 2, First Half (approximately 30 minutes)

**6. Break into Two (approximately minute 25)**
The protagonist makes a decision and enters the "new world." This is an active choice, not being pushed along.

**7. B Story**
Introduces the B Story line — typically a new relationship (love line, friendship line, mentor line). The B Story carries the thematic argument. A Plot asks "Can the protagonist achieve their goal?" B Plot asks "Can the protagonist grow?"

**8. Fun and Games (minutes 30-55)**
This is "the section that dominates the trailer." The protagonist tries old methods to solve new problems in the new world. There are successes and setbacks, but the overall rhythm is lighter.

**9. Midpoint (approximately minute 55)**
A Plot and B Plot converge here. False victory (appears to have won but it's fake) or false defeat (appears to have lost but it's a turning point). Stakes escalate — from personal level to a larger level.

### Act 2, Second Half (approximately 30 minutes)

**10. Bad Guys Close In (minutes 55-75)**
Internal and external pressure tighten simultaneously. External: antagonist/obstacle escalates. Internal: team fractures, self-doubt. The protagonist's Flaw begins to produce real costs.

**11. All Is Lost (approximately minute 75)**
The lowest point. The protagonist loses what matters most. "The whiff of death" — literal or metaphorical death. Old methods have completely failed.

**12. Dark Night of the Soul (minutes 75-85)**
The protagonist confronts their own Lie and Ghost directly. Not enlightened by others — the protagonist sees the truth themselves in the ruins. This is the threshold of arc transformation.

### Act 3 (approximately 15 minutes)

**13. Break into Three (approximately minute 85)**
The protagonist has figured it out — not an upgraded version of old methods, but a completely new approach. The realizations from A Plot and B Plot merge here.

**14. Finale (minutes 85-105)**
Five-step finale:
a. Gathering the team
b. Executing the plan
c. High tower surprise — the plan fails
d. Dig deep down — confront the crisis with the grown new self
e. Execution of the new plan

**15. Final Image**
Forms a contrast with the Opening Image. Shows the character's "new normal" — the state after change. No need to explain what the character learned. One image is worth a thousand words.
